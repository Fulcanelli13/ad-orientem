## What changed beyond keyword matching

This pass compared the **literal historical English original-work OCR context** for 24 homily passages with the **full corresponding canonical Oxford/Newman Catena excerpt text**, not merely their matched five-word sequences. The provenance-carrying registry is `dist/schaff-chrysostom-focused-parallels/focused-chrysostom-source-parallels.v1.json`. The sixteen "likely same" annotations are **not** sixteen certified duplicates and are **not** authorization to suppress or publish any words. They describe plausible source-linked similarities which still lack original-Greek collation, established complete response boundaries and human editorial certification. The six "shared wording" comparisons may represent quotations from Scripture in different contexts, even where the inherited Father label is identical.

### Why this is materially important

- **Homily XV, righteousness:** the original-work passage and Catena both distinguish righteousness understood generally from the particular virtue opposed to covetousness. Research: probably the same argument.
- **Homily XXV, priestly offering:** both explain the healed leper's offering as selective observance of the Law rather than indiscriminate abolition. Research: probably the same argument.
- **Homily XLIII, return of the unclean spirit:** both describe the more dangerous state after deliverance when someone becomes remiss. Research: probably the same argument.
- **Homily LXXXVI, Pilate's wife's dream:** both give alternative explanations for why the wife—not Pilate—received the warning; they are materially similar in the available historical English text. Research: probably the same argument.
- **Homily XXI, 'take no thought':** the original-work question is about food and bodily provision, while its Catena lexical hit concerns reliance on the Spirit when speaking. A shared phrase is not the same theological explanation.
- **Homily XXX, 'mercy and not sacrifice':** the source treats the Sabbath controversy, while the matched Catena passage treats the disciples' reaction to anointing. The same biblical saying appears in different settings.
- **Homily LXXX, 'mercy and not sacrifice':** the original-work text returns to anointing, whereas its matched Catena passage concerns the Sabbath. This is an inverse cross-pericope quotation collision, not established duplicated exposition.
- **Homily XLIX, looking heavenward:** the available original-work OCR cuts off immediately after discussing Christ's relation and equality to the Father, while the matched Catena excerpt continues into the loaves narrative. This needs a longer printed paragraph before comparing arguments.

A further misleading match appears in Homily XLVII: the source is about the disciples understanding parables, while the Catena hit occurs in the different healing of blind men. The wording `Yea, Lord` alone cannot establish shared commentary.

### What is actually verified

The focus registry contains **24** exact source-and-Catena pairs, with the historical OCR question, nearby ensuing prose, inherited Father label, original canonical excerpt, exact word-based matches, full available OCR context and SHA-256 of both source passages. Within these 24, **14** have paired printed quotation spans, **nine** have matched words inside a printed quotation, and **five** have incomplete quotation boundaries. These are real source-observation statistics.

The first-pass interpretation registry links by **source ID**, records an explanation and preserves all disclaimers: no new Father words invented, no complete-response claim, no original-Greek/Latin critical collation, no independent editorial approval and no public-release permission. Assertions verify that all 24 hypotheses resolve to real immutable source parallel records, that the source author label remains inherited/uncertified, and that every publication and novelty flag is still blocked.
