# Via Patrum benchmark — what we should reuse and what we should not

**21 October 2026 | Public-source feature audit, not a content-import license.**

Via Patrum (https://viapatrum.org/) deserves to be treated as a **prior-art reference** before we create more question taxonomies or mine complete patristic works. The public site already provides much of the experience we intended to build for Living Catena.

## Verified public capabilities

1. **Ask the Fathers — https://viapatrum.org/ask/**: free-form reader questions; answers quote selected original authors' sentences **in Via Patrum's translation**, with exact passage links. The site's current explanation says it promotes matching items from its 100 historical questions, finds nearby passages, and selects up to two sentences per work. Its own interface states nothing is added or reworded to the *retrieved quoted sentences*.
2. **Topics — https://viapatrum.org/topics/**: a broad topic taxonomy (including Scripture, God, Christ, Spirit, Salvation, Church, Last Things, and Christian life), multiple related historical witnesses organized chronologically, with longer passages accessible.
3. **Timeline — https://viapatrum.org/explore/**: 100 explicit historical doctrine questions, dated witnesses, quotations and editorial explanations; includes opposing testimony and acknowledges indirect evidence. The home page currently identifies 100 questions and 763 quotations from 179 writers/councils. These are **Via Patrum's** evidence-selection and interpretation decisions, not our approved Catholic editorial judgments.
4. **Scripture — https://viapatrum.org/scripture/matthew/1/** (also /5/, /25/): Gospel chapter/verse crosswalk to patristic passages, covering works far outside Thomas's selected *Catena Aurea*. These are usually patristic **citations of biblical verses**, not guaranteed explanations of the Gospel pericope in Aquinas's *Catena*. Matthew 1 page reported 35 passages by 12 writers, Matthew 5 330 by 23 and Matthew 25 97 by 16 at inspection.
5. **Works and sources — https://viapatrum.org/about/** and https://viapatrum.org/methodology/: currently states 372 live works and 14,059 translated sections of 20,708; modern English is translated with AI assistance and checks against Greek/Latin. The methodology explicitly says these are AI checks, not scholars' line-by-line verification, and not every work has completed every step. This is a research aid, **not a critical edition**.

## Clear overlap with Living Catena

Via Patrum has **already solved at least much of our desired question discovery, topic browsing, Bible links, sentence-level retrieval and provenance presentation problem**. Do not describe this work as an unprecedented invention; examine its solution and learn from its information architecture. This benchmark **changes priorities**: first benefit from our canonical Catena public-domain source and pre-existing Via Patrum organization; do not automatically re-mine full treatises or duplicate a generic patristic library.

## Differences that still justify Ad Orientem's Living Catena

- **Distinct base texts:** Our 814 Gospel pericopes / 12,692 English excerpts are the pinned Oxford/Newman *Catena Aurea* selection with its actual excerpt text and inherited labels. Via Patrum's general library covers hundreds of separate works; a verse crosslink there does not establish exact same quotation and does not replace Aquinas's selected commentary.
- **Messenger historical voices:** Living Catena is meant to allow conversation in an existing Messenger interface with authentic **original historical quotations**, never summarize/paraphrase Fathers as if they spoke AI text. Via Patrum's Ask section selects sentences, but its separate Timeline pages include modern editorial summaries and claims of doctrinal agreement/opposition, which should not be republished in Fathers' speech bubbles.
- **Traditional Roman Catholic context:** Our editorial context is Catholic, 1962-calendar-oriented, and tied to Gospel exegesis and the Catena's specific author labels and source references. Via Patrum includes later Protestant writers and historical denominational comparison, which can be valuable research but does not map directly onto our primary Catena experience. Critical-edition pseudepigrapha and authored/quoted voices need explicit safeguards.
- **Original text and licensing:** Via Patrum's **new English translations are its own modern creative work**, not automatically public domain, regardless of free web access. We must not scrape/bulk duplicate them without a verified compatible license or permission. Our existing Oxford Catena translation remains our original text source. Public **URLs and bibliographic metadata** may be used as research links, with source and access conditions respected.
- **Our sentence-level objective differs:** The existing Catena excerpts may themselves contain quoted objections, rhetorical questions and multiple arguments. Even when Via Patrum has indexed a shared biblical verse, we still must align the actual Father words present in *our* Catena before approving a Messenger question–answer association.

## First controlled comparison: Matthew

| Example | Our original Catena | Via Patrum public aid | Editorial decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Matthew 1:16 — why Joseph's genealogy? | `catena.matthew.1.16:14`, Jerome | https://viapatrum.org/scripture/matthew/1/ | Verse-based discovery is useful; **not evidence of an identical passage or translation** |
| Matthew 1:18 — why Mary betrothed? | `catena.matthew.1.18:3`, Jerome, with a separate Ignatius argument | Same Matthew 1 page | Keep four reasons and their distinct authorial attribution |
| Matthew 5:14–16 — hidden lamp | `catena.matthew.5.14:15`, Augustine | https://viapatrum.org/scripture/matthew/5/ | We have self-contained classical commentary; no Via Patrum import needed |
| Matthew 25:46 — eternal punishment | `catena.matthew.25.46:8`, Gregory, quoting opponent | https://viapatrum.org/scripture/matthew/25/; https://viapatrum.org/explore/ | The *historical opponent's* question must not be attributed as Gregory's own belief. Do not automatically absorb broad Timeline theological conclusions |

No current overlap percentages or claims of equal passage identities have been established; only the websites and our four exact canonical source pointers were verified.

## Work instruction for the next review

1. Maintain the **complete** 12,692-row first-pass inventory and 45 source-ordered review batches.
2. Process each batch with a two-stage method: automatic triage of all 300 pointers, then careful contextual spot review of candidate passages in manageable subsets. Never claim all 300 are semantically verified merely because they have lexical tags.
3. Store modern **editorial reader questions** separate from source text. The cited Father answer remains a direct pointer into the unaltered canonical Catena, never a fabricated response.
4. Prefer linking to Via Patrum's relevant **topic, Gospel chapter or original-source page** for editorial discovery, but do not copy its new English translation or assume its AI-built topic maps represent our precise Catena excerpts.
5. Where the Catena extract really lacks an answer, consult full historical work **selectively**. Consider Via Patrum's Greek/Latin edition references as leads and corroborate independently.
6. Do not commission, import or synthesize Messenger Father replies until original-source provenance and Catholic contextual review are complete.
