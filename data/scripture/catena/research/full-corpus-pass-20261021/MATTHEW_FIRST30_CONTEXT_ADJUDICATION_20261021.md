# Matthew — first 30 source-context adjudications (AI-assisted)

This research stage inspected the complete existing Oxford/Newman Catena source fragments for the 30 highest-priority candidates in the Matthew catalogue, with the directly adjoining author-labelled commentary where interpretation or speaker ownership warranted it. **No human source editor has approved these passages, no original-language critical editions have been collated, and none is publication eligible.**

## Findings

| Assessment | Cases | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| context dependency | 3 | This fragment by itself does not justify a standalone question-to-answer presentation |
| direct | 12 | Original excerpt actually treats the proposed question; still unapproved |
| direct with caution | 10 | The argument is present but historical voice or qualification must be preserved |
| historical claim disclosure | 1 | Patristic interpretation is not independent evidence of astronomical/geographic history |
| question revision needed | 1 | Editorial question overstates what the rhetorical fragment actually argues |
| source work or doctrine check | 3 | The Catena text is readable but demands primary work/philology or theological source review |

**Three context-dependent entries**: Matthew 13:24:14 on judging tares requires the following Augustine passage to distinguish Church discipline; Matthew 11:28:1 raises the question of promised rest but ends without completing its explanation; Matthew 12:31:10 expressly offers an alternative Chrysostom exegesis following Augustine's distinct long discussion. These must **not** be surfaced as self-sufficient direct answers.

**A question requiring revision**: Gregory, Matthew 11:28:13, rhetorically denies the burden of shunning disordered desire; the proposed editorial wording asking why Christ's yoke is described as a burden can mislead about his point. Do not treat a new paraphrase as Gregory's words.

**Source-critical escalations**: Jerome's Law/Gospel burden comparison (11:28:17), Augustine's Jeconiah numbering plus typology (1:17:9), and Jerome's Isaiah 7:14 philology (1:22:4) need original-work, theological or philological verification. Chrysostom's worldwide darkness (27:45:4) is transmitted historical interpretation, **not** confirmed natural science.

**Speaker boundaries**: Jerome's excerpts in Matthew 12:46:2, 1:24:4, and 27:51:18 report Helvidius's objections, not Jerome's own opinions. Hilary's Matthew 27:45:8 quotes theories he rejects. Distinct Chrysostom, Augustine, Hilary, Jerome and other readings in the same pericope remain separate; none becomes a composite speech bubble.

## What the tests establish

The source contract independently dereferences each of the 30 pointers from the unchanged canonical chapter packs, confirms the exact inherited source label, excerpt length, literal evidence phrase, any adjoining context pointer, and blocked publication state. It **does not** prove a historical attribution or independently certify theological correctness. All 30 records remain source-linked metadata; original commentary text is not duplicated or rewritten.

The 30 candidates are selected deterministically from the existing published research-only ranking; subsequent reviews may add records without rewriting these frozen judgments. Product integration should expose their limits and avoid presenting context-dependent passages as self-standing answer messages; it must **not** claim editorial signoff.
