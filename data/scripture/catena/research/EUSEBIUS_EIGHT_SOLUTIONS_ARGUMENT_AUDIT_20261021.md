## To Marinus I–IV — substantial source findings

| Division | Editorial problem | Source's argument variants and dependencies |
|---|---|---|
| I | Matthew 28:1 vs Mark 16:9 on timing | **I.1** presents a hypothetical manuscript-based exclusion of Mark's longer ending and an opposing hypothetical interpreter who retains it; **I.2** then conditionally solves the reading by punctuation, distinguishing resurrection from appearance. The two routes are **alternatives**, not one final editorial verdict |
| II | Matthew 28 and John 20 portray Magdalene at the tomb differently | Attempts a temporal/lexical solution, then multiple-visit sequencing. A second **different-Marys** family allows two women from Magdala and considers a possible copyist addition of the epithet. The text ultimately prefers avoiding a scribal error allegation when presenting this family; none of the suggestions is independently established history |
| III | Jesus forbids contact in John 20:17 but permits women to clasp His feet in Matthew 28:9 | Under the **same-Mary** hypothesis, a sequence of return visits and two appearances resolves the difference; the prohibition is also read spiritually in relation to Mary's recognition of Christ's risen divinity. The alternate chronology and Mary-identity problems from II must remain attached |
| IV | One/two angels, inside/outside tomb and one young man across four Gospels | Distinguishes appearances by time/place/witnesses. **Significant transmission warning:** published editorial commentary indicates that IV may come from another epitomator and IV.5–6 may belong to an unpreserved question. In a distinct supplementary witness, the inferred sequence of angel sightings *differs* from the IV.1 epitome. Do not silently harmonize these divergent witnesses |

Online passage anchors: [Marinus I](https://catholiclibrary.org/library/view?chunk.id=00000003&docId=%2FFathers-Synchronized-EN%2FEusebius_Caesariensis__Quaestiones_evangelicae_ad_Marinum.en.html), [Marinus II](https://catholiclibrary.org/library/view?chunk.id=00000009&docId=Fathers-Synchronized-EN%2FEusebius_Caesariensis__Quaestiones_evangelicae_ad_Marinum.en.html), [Marinus III](https://catholiclibrary.org/library/view?chunk.id=00000011&docId=%2FFathers-Synchronized-EN%2FEusebius_Caesariensis__Quaestiones_evangelicae_ad_Marinum.en.html), [supplement V including angel comparison](https://catholiclibrary.org/library/view?chunk.id=00000007&docId=%2FFathers-Synchronized-EN%2FEusebius_Caesariensis__Supplementa_ad_quaestiones_ad_Marinum.en.html).

**Caution on Greek editorial integrity:** the online synced text has page-number/OCR artefacts. The full IV argument and paragraph boundary have **not** passed primary-edition/folio review; the structure registry marks these spans **partial**, with no fabricated exact-Greek quote. The 2010 printed Greek/English edition includes an explicit warning that IV.5–6 may come from a different lost question, but collation of that footnote against the physical/digitized edition is pending.

## To Stephanus I–IV — substantial source findings

| Division | Source treatment | Evidential and authorship caveats |
|---|---|---|
| I | Why Christ's genealogy is reported via Joseph despite His birth from Mary and the Holy Spirit | The objection is **not the author's assertion** of Joseph's biological fatherhood. The source discusses the social perception of Jesus as Joseph's son and its narrative/divine-economy purpose, while preserving the virginal conception. Further source paragraph mapping pending |
| II | Matthew begins with Abraham and descends; Luke rises through Adam toward God | Different authorial ordering and scriptural genealogy conventions; interpretation is bound to the theological concern with Christ and spiritual rebirth |
| III | Solomon/Jacob vs Nathan/Heli and both called Joseph's ancestors | An early proposed **grammatical and public-opinion** account contrasts Luke's "as was supposed" with Matthew's explicit lineage; another and different account reads Luke's ancestry in connection with baptism and regeneration. They must not be merged into one proven biological genealogy |
| IV | Africanus's argument concerning the same genealogical difficulty | The heading **identifies Julius Africanus**. He rejects baseless priest-king genealogy constructions and transmits a levirate/natural-versus-legal-fatherhood explanation. Eusebius is a transmitter; quoted **Africanus** speech stays Africanus speech. The Africanus passage in *Church History* I.7 is a related but independently transmitted form |

## Production-specific embargoes

1. The registry contains **editorial notes only**. The existing Messenger must not display them as `speaker: Eusebius` or `speaker: Africanus` messages.
2. The literal question of a correspondent, Eusebius's narration, hypothetical objectors, biblical speakers, and Africanus as quoted author must remain separate.
3. Marinus IV.5–6 is **not available** as a certified full answer to question IV; do not merge an orphaned answer fragment into the angels topic merely because it appears under the same inherited heading.
4. Distinct arguments about Mark's ending are **early textual-critical observations** and alternative reading proposals. They do not direct the app to remove any Scripture passage.
5. Claims about two Magdalene women, textual scribal errors, the exact chronology of the Resurrection encounters, and the genealogies remain attributed **historical hypotheses**, not compulsory Catholic teaching.
6. Independent Greek-witness collation, translation license determination, theological review, and continuous-context excerpt approval are mandatory before publication.
