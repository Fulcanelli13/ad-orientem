## B. Compare the actual biblical wording

### Joseph is sent to his brothers

**Existing text (Genesis 37:12–14):**

And when his brethren abode in Sechem, feeding their father’s flocks, Israel said to him: Thy brethren feed the sheep in Sichem: come, I will send thee to them. And when he answered: I am ready: he said to him: Go, and see if all things be well with thy brethren, and the cattle: and bring me word again what is doing. So being sent from the vale of Hebron, he came to Sichem:

**Proposed text (Genesis 37:13–14):**

Israel said to him: Thy brethren feed the sheep in Sichem: come, I will send thee to them. And when he answered: I am ready: he said to him: Go, and see if all things be well with thy brethren, and the cattle: and bring me word again what is doing. So being sent from the vale of Hebron, he came to Sichem:

**Assessment:** Verse 13 repeats the sheep-tending at Sichem in verse 12; the direct command and Joseph's reply stay intact. Mild historical-geography loss only.

### Joseph reveals God's providence

**Existing text (Genesis 45:1–11):**

Joseph could no longer refrain himself before many that stood by: whereupon he commanded that all should go out, and no stranger be present at their knowing one another. And he lifted up his voice with weeping, which the Egyptians, and all the house of Pharao heard. And he said to his brethren: I am Joseph: Is my father yet living? His brethren could not answer him, being struck with exceeding great fear. And he said mildly to them: Come nearer to me. And when they were come near him, he said: I am Joseph, your brother, whom you sold into Egypt. Be not afraid, and let it not seem to you a hard case that you sold me into these countries: for God sent me before you into Egypt for your preservation. For it is two years since the famine began to be upon the land, and five years more remain, wherein there can be neither ploughing nor reaping. And God sent me before, that you may be preserved upon the earth, and may have food to live. Not by your counsel was I sent hither, but by the will of God: who hath made me as it were a father to Pharao, and lord of his whole house, and governor in all the land of Egypt. Make haste, and go ye up to my father, and say to him: Thus saith thy son Joseph: God hath made me lord of the whole land of Egypt; come down to me, linger not. And thou shalt dwell in the land of Gessen: and thou shalt be near me, thou and thy sons, and thy sons’ sons, thy sheep, and thy herds, and all things that thou hast. And there I will feed thee, (for there are yet five years of famine remaining) lest both thou perish, and thy house, and all things that thou hast.

**Proposed text (Genesis 45:1–5; Genesis 45:8–11):**

Joseph could no longer refrain himself before many that stood by: whereupon he commanded that all should go out, and no stranger be present at their knowing one another. And he lifted up his voice with weeping, which the Egyptians, and all the house of Pharao heard. And he said to his brethren: I am Joseph: Is my father yet living? His brethren could not answer him, being struck with exceeding great fear. And he said mildly to them: Come nearer to me. And when they were come near him, he said: I am Joseph, your brother, whom you sold into Egypt. Be not afraid, and let it not seem to you a hard case that you sold me into these countries: for God sent me before you into Egypt for your preservation. Not by your counsel was I sent hither, but by the will of God: who hath made me as it were a father to Pharao, and lord of his whole house, and governor in all the land of Egypt. Make haste, and go ye up to my father, and say to him: Thus saith thy son Joseph: God hath made me lord of the whole land of Egypt; come down to me, linger not. And thou shalt dwell in the land of Gessen: and thou shalt be near me, thou and thy sons, and thy sons’ sons, thy sheep, and thy herds, and all things that thou hast. And there I will feed thee, (for there are yet five years of famine remaining) lest both thou perish, and thy house, and all things that thou hast.

**Assessment:** Joseph's identity, forgiveness, divine providence, invitation to Jacob and land of Gessen remain verbatim. Verses 6–7 explicitly articulate famine duration and preservation; verse 5 retains preservation and verse 11 repeats five years. The shorter sequence remains an editorial judgment.

### Peter's journey to Cornelius

**Existing text (Acts 10:21–23):**

Then Peter, going down to the men, said: Behold, I am he whom you seek. What is the cause for which you are come? Who said: Cornelius, a centurion, a just man and one that feareth God, and having good testimony from all the nation of the Jews, received an answer of an holy angel, to send for thee into his house And to hear words of thee. Then bringing them in, he lodged them. And the day following, he arose and went with them: and some of the brethren from Joppe accompanied him.

**Proposed text (omit, with adjoining Scripture unchanged):**

*Omitted entirely in this candidate; adjacent verses are unchanged.*

**Assessment:** The Spirit's instruction in 10:19–20 flows into Peter's arrival at Caesarea in 10:24; Cornelius's own explanatory reply in 10:30–32 remains. Removes Peter's reception of the messengers and accompanying Christians; assess the missing transition between Acts 10:20 and 10:24.

**Immediate literary priority:** H06 (wickedness before the Flood), H50 (Christ in the Temple), H61 (selected Farewell Discourses) and H74 (the opening vision of Revelation). They are source trials, not automatic additions.

**Optional material:** H27 (Judges' named deliverers), H42 (Daniel as an exilic companion) and H55 (the Samaritan woman). These are worthwhile in their own right but are not automatically required for the covenant narrative.

**Separate genealogy:** H45 remains a dedicated navigation thread using Matthew 1:1–17 and Luke 3:23–38 as distinct witnesses, not a single merged genealogy and not 574 extra compulsory narrative words.

## D. Length and integrity

Three plausible cuts would save 151 words. All four high-priority missing-source trials together would add 1,154 words in the existing Douay witness, for a net increase of approximately 1,003 words. This is why the aim is an economical *faithful* story, not the smallest possible word count.

Source integrity remains uncollated against printed editions. Final acceptance requires a genuinely consecutive literary read, Scripture-context and theological review, and special care at source discontinuities. Parallels remain outside scope.
