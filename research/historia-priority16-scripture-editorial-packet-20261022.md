## Criteria for approving any single insertion

1. **Source-ownership:** the original words must continue to come exclusively from the pinned Catholic Scripture witness in the canonical reader. The explicit ranges are navigation data, not a new copy of the text.
2. **Completeness before shortness:** retain a whole passage if narrowing it would sever the speaker, audience, reason for the response, sacramental significance or historical causal chain. This is why Emmaus (Luke 24:13–35), the washing of feet (John 13:1–17) and several other Gospel scenes remain unabridged.
3. **Explicit omissions:** wherever discontiguous parts are proposed, the reader must show a source-reference break and provide access to the complete text; it must not visually rejoin the verses into a purportedly continuous quotation.
4. **Narrative economy:** decide whether the covenantal arc genuinely needs the addition. A famous or important passage is not automatically suitable for the economical Creation-to-New-Creation backbone.
5. **Chronological, canonical and genre safeguards:** preserve the distinct sources at the Last Supper and Calvary; keep the Patmos event and heavenly vision in Revelation's literary order; cross-reference biblical typology only in the separate thematic compendium.
6. **Historical edition and content collation:** the present tests confirm transcribed witness availability and identity across repository rows, **not** correspondence to definitive printed editions or final source-critical approval.

## Particular integrity holds

**Daniel 3 (nineteen selected verses from one hundred):** the proposed 3:12–23, 3:49–50 and 3:91–95 excerpt skips the Catholic Greek prayer of Azarias and the hymn (3:24–90). These are **not omitted because they are noncanonical**. The research reader must disclose the full omission and retain an obvious route to the complete chapter. This is the highest-risk economical reduction.

**Numbers 13–14 (twenty-nine selected verses):** the scouts’ report, the people's refusal, the faithful witnesses, Moses' intercession and the desert consequence are represented. In particular, the source gaps may not cause the divine pardon and subsequent sentence to be depicted without the intervening petition.

**Joshua 23–24:** these are **two distinct addresses**, not a composed Joshua speech. The proposed land-entry covenant renewal belongs *before* the already selected transition into Judges in the mixed HS-M11 movement.

**2 Maccabees 7:** the abridgment retains a son’s resurrection witness, the mother’s exhortation and the deaths, but omits other martyrdoms and dialogue. A publication editor must assess whether the moral, historical and textual integrity survives that reduction.

**Exodus 16, Nehemiah 8:** partial readings preserve the divine provision / public covenantal hearing, respectively, rather than simply taking isolated memorable quotations.
