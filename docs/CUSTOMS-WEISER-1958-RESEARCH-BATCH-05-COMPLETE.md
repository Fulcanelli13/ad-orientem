## Highest-value source findings

1. **Name days and parish patronal feasts** (pp. 279–281): the baptismal patron's feast is historically kept with Mass, modest presents, a family meal and sometimes release from ordinary chores; a French `fête patronale` includes public religious and village observance. This is a promising family/saints interface, not an invitation to duplicate feast dates or promote calendar rank.
2. **Assumption processions** (pp. 289–291): French cities and towns carry images of the Assumption; French Alpine clergy are also described blessing mountain pastures and livestock. Weiser separately records herbs and fruit blessings; proposed `BLSS-002` in outstanding PR #1512 must be reused, not duplicated. Locality and priestly rite need independent closure.
3. **French Nativity of Mary grape blessing** (pp. 305–306; **8 September**): vineyard communities bring ripe grapes to church for a blessing and hold a meal with the new harvest. Distinct from August 15 herb blessing; strong French-world candidate with historical attestation, not proof that every church in France does this.
4. **Candlemas** (pp. 297–300): decorated candles are blessed and kept at home, including beside the sick or dying. Canonical `DOM-011` already owns the domestic custom; do not create duplicate practice. Unverified storm, lightning and wolf repulsion claims are not Catholic guarantees.
5. **All Saints and All Souls** (pp. 310–314): cemetery visits and priestly grave blessings; home Candlemas candle during evening prayers; distinctive **Breton overnight bell and farm visits**; Polish `Wypominki` names of the dead; unofficial family 'Soul Nights'; soul breads distributed to poor and children. `DEAD-001`, `DEAD-003`, `BELL-004` and `DOM-011` are existing owner candidates. Do not invent a liturgical octave for All Souls.
6. **St Blaise (3 February)** (p. 317): traditional crossed-candle throat blessing, already held under `BLSS-001`. A separate historical `pan bendito` blessed bread/sticks practice is geographically bounded; not a replacement for a priest's formula. Legendary healing stories never become medical claims.
7. **St Joseph (19 March)** (pp. 323–325): small festival breads, charity of the St Joseph table, northern Spanish pilgrim meal, Valencia historical `Fallas`. Separate spiritual charity from locally adopted carnival/civic observances and from 1958 disciplinary dispensations.
8. **St Anthony of Padua (13 June)** (pp. 325–328): "St Anthony's Bread" as almsgiving organised under the name at **Toulon, France, in 1890**; requests for lost items and Tuesday devotions; Lisbon household shrines. Prefer contextual charitable practice and existing Saints/Pray owners, not a new novena library.
9. **St John the Baptist (24 June)** (pp. 328–331): Europe-wide midsummer fires with specific **Breton fishing-boat** celebrations and local prayers. The described dangerous fires, wheel rolling, charms and claims of protection should be historical research only, not recommended activities.
10. **St Christopher (25 July)** (pp. 334–336): places blessing cars on the saint's feast, and traditional driver medals. A blessing requires a legitimate priestly sacramental witness and local availability; no guarantee against accidents.
11. **St Anne (26 July)** (pp. 336–338): historical patronage of mothers/families, pilgrimage to St Anne de Beaupré, and regional Anne name-day celebrations. Existing pilgrimage and Saints architecture should own much of the context.
12. **St Nicholas and Lucy** (pp. 339–343): early Advent visits of a bishop-clothed St Nicholas who catechises and gives modest gifts; St Lucy's Scandinavian breakfast and household lights. These are regional Catholic/adopted Christian practices, not prescribed universal Roman rites. The Scandinavian Lucy folklore contains pre-Christian winter-magic strands and fire hazards—do not instruct families to use actual burning crowns.

## Important source-criticism and date locks

- **St Blaise 3 February and St Lucy 13 December.** The Scribd OCR produces misleading heading readings "Blaise (February 8)" and "Lucy (December 18)" at two locations while the text itself and established feast calendar identify the correct dates. Protect against propagating OCR errors into Calendar or Saints records.
- Weiser often relies on *legends* when discussing Christopher, Nicholas, Patrick, Valentine and other saints. Preserve him as a 1958 historical witness of devotion; do not elevate hagiographic tales to attested biography.
- His statements about *1917 Code obligations, vigils, indulgences or ceremonial details* are contextual. Use separate competent sources for the **1962 liturgical calendar and Ritual** and for **present law**.
- `Saint Christopher vehicle blessings`, `Mary grape blessings`, `Blaise throats` and `Anne pilgrimage` are different classes of observation; do not collapse priestly sacramentals into domestic actions or infer physical place coordinates.
- Weiser's unqualified claims about the origin of Halloween and St Valentine's Day may be contested by other scholarship. Retain as an author's historical hypotheses rather than asserting certainty.
- The feast-day customs' *seasonal timing* (spring and autumn planting, European harvests, Swedish winter) does not transfer climatically to Mauritius or the Southern Hemisphere. Calendar dates and cultural context must remain separate.
