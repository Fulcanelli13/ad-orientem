## Source-critical editorial corrections and qualifications

1. **Santa Croce Camerina (p.451):** the three costumed poor visitors returned from Mass and had received a `speciale benedizione`. The *later* vernacular formula is spoken by the costumed person playing Joseph, **not** proof that this performance is an authorized Catholic priestly blessing. The alleged harm to those eating early is explicitly a popular warning (p.450), not doctrine.
2. **Ribera (p.447):** using laurel from the *stràgula* in hot water for stomach pain is a reported domestic belief; no approved blessing or therapeutic evidence is established.
3. **Catania (pp.225, 228):** Pitrè gives an explicit 1641 citation to **Pietro Carrera**; Pitrè's use of the 1834 image likewise proves description by Pitrè, not direct inspection of the historical source in this pass. Keep distinct-author originals on hold.
4. **Syracuse (pp.273, 278–279):** the St Lucy May bird release is explicit in the original text; the printed description also observes birds captured/injured. The 1881 reference is Pitrè's **own earlier work**, never independent corroboration. Famine ships remain a legendary origin story.
5. **Melilli (p.290):** `Dicono pure` introduces the reported earlier dramatic/oratorio custom, not proof of an approved Roman Rite or that those performances still continued in 1900. Crescimanno 1899 remains a separate unread work.
6. **Palazzolo/Solarino (pp.356–359):** popular claims of curse/insect, statue sweat, snakes and water effects must remain folklore. A further passage on original p.360 (PDF 418) explicitly says that tongue-on-floor penitents continued **against categorical ecclesiastical opposition**; it would be particularly erroneous to recommend the practice.
7. **Naro/Girgenti (pp.375, 377, 382):** Pitrè distinguishes vivid popular accounts and an attributed contribution from priest Giuseppe Russo; later clinical/relic claims are not medical facts.
8. **Caltanissetta (pp.516–517):** the nine-year bread-and-water fast on Easter Monday, nine blessed candles for deathbed use, late September feast and weather/face omens belong to different categories; Pitrè's record of a blessing does **not** identify an approved 1962 ritual formulary.
9. **Scicli (p.340):** `messa cantata due ore dopo la mezzanotte` is reported by Pitrè, as is `Ci volle un breve del papa`. The **papal breve itself remains unread and unauthenticated**. The cited 1881 *Spettacoli* text is same-author reuse.
10. **Messina (pp.147, 161, 165, 167):** discontinued commercial fair blessing, former live child performers on the Vara, miracle report quoted from older material, and belief that the platform moved unaided are separate historic assertions, not liturgical directions.
11. **Calatafimi (p.504):** the sacred allegorical procession was explicitly **not annual** when means were lacking. Pitrè mentions an older 1780 *Relazione* and other printed accounts but these prior pamphlets are **not directly read**.
