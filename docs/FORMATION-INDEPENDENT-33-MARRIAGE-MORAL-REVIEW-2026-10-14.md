## Proportionate citation verification — governing rule

A properly attributed, accurately reproduced passage with an identifiable work, exact page or section, and a reliable original or credible intermediary is **sufficient for the specific argument it actually records**. The author’s complete book is **not a mandatory acquisition or certification gate**. Consult additional surrounding context only if wording, meaning, selection or attribution materially affects the answer. A reliable quotation published by a critic may establish the quoted words while leaving any unquoted, broader reconstruction expressly unverified.

Record **accepted targeted citation**, **context spot-check required**, or **attribution insufficient** per claim. Do not hold an otherwise well-supported Q&A indefinitely for full-book retrieval, do not confuse opponent-quote verification with endorsement of that argument, and retain inline linked citations and distinct opposing voices. The independent Q&A audit still checks actual answers and the accuracy and proportionality of cited claims.

## High-value CSE findings without wholesale rewrites

The reviewed short answers generally preserve useful distinctions. [Aquinas II-II q.154 aa.4–5](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3154.htm) confirms that kisses/touches are not by their species necessarily mortal sins, and that sleep-related involuntary emissions are not themselves culpable, while separately evaluating preceding voluntary causes (**CSE027,033,034**). [II-II q.152 a.4](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3152.htm) distinguishes an objectively superior consecrated state from the holiness of particular married people (**CSE028**). [II-II q.169 aa.1–2](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3169.htm) recognises variation in clothing conventions and attention to intent (**CSE030**).

The original [Code of Canon Law](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib4-cann998-1165_en.html) supports the distinction between an engagement promise and matrimonial consent (**CSE032**), actual sterility versus the impediment of impotence (**CSE044**), and the separation/nullity/dissolution distinctions (**CSE049–050**). No source should be used to imply that an unsafe spouse needs prior permission before leaving immediate danger. The [original qualitative priest study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25617141/) confirms only reports from 15 participants and cannot establish universal psychological effects or prevalence (**CSE029**).

**CSE037 requires extra caution:** [Aquinas II-II q.43](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3043.htm) deals with scandal, not the complete technical moral-manual account of an individual proximate occasion. One source may not be presented as proving both. This was corrected in the research crosswalk.
