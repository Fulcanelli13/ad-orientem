## Cross-module reconciliation

**Mass and Special Days** own the 1962 ceremonies, including the Ascension Paschal-candle rubric, Pentecost liturgical texts and Corpus Christi processions. An actually occurring Corpus Christi procession must be selected via existing *followingAction* or other explicit ritual selection; date alone does not activate a procession. Weiser's optional station rites require independent 1962 rubrical evidence.

**Calendar** owns dates and contextual practices, not a second Mass resolver. Corpus Christi is a Thursday in the traditional Roman calendar; current transferred-date/obligation policies belong in separate calendar discipline, not in Weiser research.

**Customs Atlas** owns geographically sourced family traditions. No additional canonical custom IDs are minted here. No invented map pins, universal French custom claims or 1958 indulgence grants.

**Explore and Saints** own physical places, historical confraternities and the St Martin biography/feast. Do not insert folklore place descriptions as verified church/relic profiles.

**PR #1512** separately holds Assumption herb blessings (`BLSS-002`), St John wine (`BLSS-003`) and Easter foods (`BLSS-004`). The new 15 August harvest processions/flower and produce customs must be reconciled with that research, not duplicated.

## Evidence quality and limitations

The labels are extracted from Weiser's directly accessible book text. Some historical claims are his summaries of earlier secondary sources, hearsay or folklore. In particular, his comparisons of ancient and popular imagery need original-source and regional verification before publication. Page anchors are exact printed-page estimates checked against chapter spans; the new records also include online full-text line locators for re-review. **No independent field verification** of 87 practices is claimed.

Avoid importing the book's language about old holydays of obligation, devotional indulgences or annual Communion deadlines as current universal law. Weiser himself marks many customs as discontinued, pre-Christian or superstitious. The text on American Thanksgiving is predominantly *non-Catholic civic history*, not a Roman Rite November celebration. Sacred-object blessings are minister-specific, and the historic French harvest sheaf is not self-evidently a blessed item.
