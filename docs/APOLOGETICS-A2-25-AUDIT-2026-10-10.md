## Direct corrections to the existing questions

**APOL-036 — Sacred images.** The opposition is now <https://westminsterstandards.org/westminster-larger-catechism/> Question 109, not a generic claim that Protestants dislike pictures. It expressly restricts images of God and all religious worship not instituted by God. The Catholic reply retains Exodus 20 and 25, the Catechism and Trent, and must be evaluated for precise distinctions between image, veneration and adoration.

**APOL-037 — Relics.** The documented opposing text is <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/32136>, Calvin's *Traité des reliques* (1543, English historical translation). The rebuttal distinguishes objectively dubious relic provenance from the theological permissibility of venerating *genuine* relics under Trent XXV. Calvin's objections are not treated as manufactured or stupid.

**APOL-041 — Hell.** Westminster agrees with Catholic teaching that punishment is eternal, so it cannot represent the denial of that claim. The live opposing source now includes the <https://adventist.org/beliefs/official/millennium-and-the-end-of-sin> official Seventh-day Adventist teaching on final destruction, contrasted with Matthew 25:46 and Catholic doctrine, preserving the original Westminster historical context.

**APOL-050 — Objective morality and natural law.** The old canonical answer had drifted into transhumanism and artificial intelligence. Its opposing philosophy now uses original author texts: [J. L. Mackie, *Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong* ch. 1](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/592b5bbfd482e9898c67fd98/t/5d04218b546f150001b5b1e0/1560551819689/mackie.pdf) and [Gilbert Harman, *Moral Relativism Defended*](https://filosoficas.unam.mx/docs/545/files/Harman.pdf) against Aquinas I–II q.94 a.2. The reader distinguishes Mackie's error theory from Harman's *restricted* agreement-relative 'inner ought' thesis (Harman does **not** simply deny all objective moral comparisons). Older transhumanist research remains in the indexed research bank; contemporary AI applications are secondary to the actual natural-law question.

The primary texts were selectively inspected, not certified claim by claim. All new EN/FR prose is still a draft.
