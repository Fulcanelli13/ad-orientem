## Corrective boundaries
The canonical alias families are keyed exclusively by observed IDs, not search strings, translated names or a hardcoded civil feast date. Saturday BVM additionally verifies that the resolved date is actually a Saturday; the actual selected Common Mass remains unchanged. They change neither observed day source identity nor active Proper text, Mass rank/colour, reading sequence or commemorations. **Only the Calendar’s visible title changes.** EN/FR output and failure/unknown fail-closed behavior are contract tested. The special 2026 Pentecost Vigil composition and 2024/2027 datum are not conflated. ## Canonical source-week lineage of the 55 generic ferias

The [2024/2027 full-year reconciliation worklist](../data/calendar/1962-editorial-reconciliation-2024-2027.v1.json) records **29** dates in 2024 and **26** in 2027. **53** of the 55 have one matching IV-class `tempora:Epi*/Quadp*/Pasc*/Pent*` source weekday ID. Their English/French Calendar headlines derive the weekday and Sunday family from that observed identity, with a precise weekday/date consistency guard. They do **not** derive the Proper from a translated-title regex, turn a weekday into a Sunday, or change the Mass text.

The other **two dates** — **7 and 8 January 2027** — have no Temporale candidate. The pinned `DayResolver.inferObservance` explicitly walks backward to the **6 January Epiphany** principal observation. The projected label requires a successfully resolved **`Sancti/01-06` inherited Mass Proper**, an IV-class white observed feria and the Jan 7–12 rubrical period; **date alone is insufficient**. Compare [Missale Online](https://missale.online/festkalender/en/2027/druck) (*Weekday after Epiphany*) and [GCatholic](https://gcatholic.org/calendar/2027/Extraordinary-en) (Epiphany feria).

**Six dates** near November 2024/2027 inherit a `tempora:Epi4-*` source weekday when an impeded Epiphany Sunday is assigned after XXIII Sunday after Pentecost by [1960 General Rubrics, n.18](https://isidore.co/divinum/www/horas/Help/Rubrics/General%20Rubrics.html). Labels acknowledge the *transferred Fourth Sunday after Epiphany* and avoid falsely treating it as an ordinary January week or inventing the variable Pentecost-week number from the raw weekday ID.

The regression gates check all 55 actual DayResolver dates, both languages, exact canonical weekday/proper identities, resumed-source cases, false-positive guards, and continued rank/colour/commemoration ownership. **This certifies the bounded headline-projection rules, not the 731 Mass Propers or local particular calendars.** [Implementation PR #764](https://github.com/Fulcanelli13/ad-orientem/pull/764).

### 4 December — mass-prayer source-owner repair

Both independent 1962 witnesses, a [full 1962 Ordo entry](https://ordo.fsspx.org/day/2026-12-04?lang=en), and [dated Latin prayer sections](https://missale.online/kalender/en/2026-12-04) give **the principal III-class Peter Chrysologus Mass, the privileged Advent III-class feria, and the ordinary St Barbara Virgin-and-Martyr commemoration**. This is **three Collects, three Secrets, three Postcommunions**, not simply three day labels. It recurs wherever this weekday feast is observed.

The pinned shared resolver already composes Advent prayers; the donor's universal Sanctoral ID set omitted St Barbara. In [PR #767](https://github.com/Fulcanelli13/ad-orientem/pull/767), her commemoration is attached **only to the canonical principal Peter Chrysologus identity** and draws its Latin named Collect from the [pinned St Barbara Proper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DivinumOfficium/divinum-officium/126a07f91ede04664108abb6fb20ace3f4de14b9/web/www/missa/Latin/Sancti/12-04pl.txt), with Common-of-a-Virgin-Martyr Secret and Postcommunion. That source file belongs to a particular-calendar source branch but its **prayer** is also attested independently in the 1962 Roman Mass witness; importing that prayer does **not** import its Polish local-calendar rank. The proper substitutions spell out *Barbaræ* in the Secret (genitive) and *Barbara* in the Postcommunion (ablative), instead of exposing the raw Common's `N.` slot.

The pinned donor does not supply a bilingual St Barbara Collect; the corresponding English and French are *editorially translated from its Latin*. They are not misrepresented as historic approved vernacular translations. The liturgical texts, language slots, three prayer groups and non-Sunday suppression are checked by the real browser Mass-composition test. A source-reader failure must remain visible, not be counted as a certification.

### 25 January and 22 February — inseparable Apostles are not optional labels

The 2024/2027 audit uncovered four additional prayer omissions that the historical first-source *commemoration count* had concealed. The shared `day.commemorations` contained the reciprocal Apostle's identity on the **Conversion of St Paul (25 January)** and **Chair of St Peter (22 February)**, but the actual Mass Proper contained *none* of that Apostle's Collect/Secret/Postcommunion content. The donor's normalized `Sancti/01-25c` and `Sancti/02-22c` are local-only paths: the source loader requires an upstream corresponding file and silently swallowed the failed load with a warning.

The [1960 General Rubrics n.110](https://isidore.co/divinum/www/horas/Help/Rubrics/General%20Rubrics.html) prescribe the reciprocal Apostle **under one conclusion**. Hence January 25 requires *one joined apostolic prayer group* (Paul + Peter) at each of Collect, Secret, Postcommunion, not two independent conclusions. February 22 likewise needs a *single joined apostolic group* (Peter + Paul), **plus a separate privileged Lenten feria** (thus two prayer groups, not three). The source-first implementation retrieves the [pinned canonical named orations from `Sancti/02-22`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DivinumOfficium/divinum-officium/126a07f91ede04664108abb6fb20ace3f4de14b9/web/www/missa/Latin/Sancti/02-22.txt) for all three languages, joins them to the principal Apostle in the same three prayer objects with one liturgical conclusion, and records their `underOneConclusion` provenance. It retains the existing Lenten commemoration and original Mass rank/colour.

**Do not** declare the four cases corrected solely because their Apostle labels appear on a Calendar card, or by appending an illicit extra independent prayer. Verify both Apostle texts and the exact 1-versus-2 prayer counts in the real production reader.
