## Six major research results

### I. The Preface dialogue has an early, specifically attested witness

Cyprian [*De oratione dominica* §31](https://catholiclibrary.org/library/view?chunk.id=00000067&docId=Synchronized-EN%2Fanf.Cyprian.OntheLordsPrayer.en.html) recounts a priest calling the assembly to lift up its heart, answered by the people. He uses this to require focused, undistracted prayer. This gives an **ancient direct witness to Sursum corda**, without asserting that Cyprian knew the exact 1962 Preface sequence. [Aquinas III q.83 a.4 ad 5](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4083.htm) cites Cyprian for this same purpose. [Guéranger PREFACE](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/) gives a later French explanation of the dialogue as shared gratitude and participation.

**French explanation (new synthesis, unpublished):** « Le dialogue de la Préface invite les fidèles à élever leur cœur vers le Seigneur avant la grande action de grâces. Saint Cyprien attestait déjà cet appel dans l'assemblée chrétienne, pour rappeler que la prière exige un cœur attentif à Dieu. Cette ancienneté ne prouve pas que toutes les formules de la Préface romaine de 1962 existaient alors mot pour mot. »

### II. The Sanctus joins two heavenly visions; the Benedictus invokes a third narrative

[Guéranger SANCTUS](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/) compares Isaiah 6:3 and Apocalypse 4:8 for the triple acclamation; the Benedictus / Hosanna come from the Palm Sunday entrance of Christ (Matthew 21:9; compare Ps 117:25–26 in Vulgate). Neither part should be flattened into a single continuous 'biblical scene'. The wording of Isaiah's Clementine Deus exercituum differs from the Mass's Deus Sabaoth. The Gloria/Angel song is yet another, distinct event. In 1962 these elements occur together in the liturgical Sanctus, with sung performance separable from the priest's recitation.

**French explanation (new synthesis, unpublished):** « Le Sanctus unit notre louange à celle du ciel : Isaïe entend les séraphins acclamer trois fois le Seigneur, tandis que saint Jean décrit dans l'Apocalypse une autre vision de la liturgie céleste. Avec le Benedictus, l'Église reprend aussi l'acclamation qui accueillit Jésus entrant à Jérusalem. Les trois récits bibliques sont distincts, mais leurs paroles sont rassemblées dans une seule louange liturgique. »

### III. 1962-specific exceptions within the Canon are an essential research result

The [actual 1962 Canon](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt) has Christmas, Epiphany, Easter, Ascension and Pentecost forms of Communicantes; [Maundy Thursday](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt) also changes the introductory paragraph of Communicantes and specifically changes Qui pridie with 'hoc est, hodie'. The [same rubrics](https://isidore.co/divinum/www/horas/Help/Rubrics/Missal1960%20rubrics.html) and [O'Connell (Canon exceptions)](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/d-The%20Celebration%20of%20Mass-%20O%27Connel%201962_OCR.txt) explicitly mention special Hanc igitur at Easter/Pentecost and Maundy Thursday. **Thus a single fixed 'Canon' formation translation is materially incomplete even before the four-slot chant Proper research.** The changes are overlays on the existing Canon text owner, not additional Mass cards. Exact applicability must follow original calendar and rubrics; don't use Gihr's older seasonal calendar.

### IV. Words of institution, Mysterium fidei, and the difference between Scripture and Roman formula

[1962 Missal p.307](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt) contains chalice words **mysterium fidei within** the priest's consecration text. Matthew, Mark, Luke and 1 Corinthians transmit institution wording with real differences, but no Gospel narrative says all the received Latin formula in precisely the same order. Guéranger explains this by tradition, yet his strong unsupported claim that all differences reproduce unheard apostolic spoken words is **historical hypothesis, not directly provable from the Scriptures**. For comparison only, [Paul VI's 1969 constitution](https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/apost_constitutions/documents/hf_p-vi_apc_19690403_missale-romanum.html) explicitly says these words were taken **out of** the words of the Lord and became the introduction to the acclamation: this is a **different rite/revision**, and its chalice wording must never be back-imported into the 1962 Mass.

**French explanation (new synthesis, unpublished):** « Les paroles de la consécration se rapportent aux récits de l'institution transmis par les Évangiles et saint Paul. Le Canon romain reçu les exprime dans sa propre forme liturgique. Ainsi, en 1962, les mots Mysterium fidei appartiennent à la formule prononcée sur le calice, bien qu'ils ne se trouvent pas dans les récits évangéliques de l'institution. Ce lien est authentiquement scripturaire et liturgique, sans être une transcription intégrale d'un seul Évangile. »

### V. Ancient textual witness changes the historical story, without changing doctrine

[De sacramentis IV.6.27](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/De_sacramentis_(Ambrosius)) quotes anamnesis, heavenly altar and Abel/Abraham/Melchisedech in a prayer whose order is **heavenly altar, then three sacrifices**; the received [1962 Roman Canon pp.308–309](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt) is **three sacrifices (Supra quae), then heavenly altar (Supplices)**. The ancient quotation says per manus angelorum tuorum (plural); the received prayer says per manus sancti Angeli tui (singular). [DTC](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_de_th%C3%A9ologie_catholique/MESSE_VIII._La_messe_dans_la_liturgie_7._Le_De_sacramentis_du_Pseudo_Ambroise) warns the early excerpt is reproduced by an author in a didactic treatise, not a surviving 1962 Missal copy, and discusses textual history. This comparison disproves blanket claim of word-for-word unbroken 1962 Canon since the 4th century, while supporting meaningful early affinity.

[Aquinas III q.83 a.4 ad 9](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4083.htm) insists the species and Christ's true body are NOT literally transported to heaven by that angel; he relates the prayer to Christ's mystical body and offerings of the faithful, citing Apocalypse 8:4. The Bible Theatre and visual arts must depict no mechanism of sacramental transport to a physical sky location.

### VI. Names, calendar episodes, silence and gestures need different authority classes

[Canon pp.304–309](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt) explicitly names the Mother of God, Joseph, apostles, martyrs and (in Supra quae) Abel/Abraham/Melchisedech. Some saints are only known in later Church history: do **not** assign a 'Bible genealogy/episode' to post-biblical historical martyrs, even though their names occur in the Canon. The 1962 [Ritus servandus IX §3](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt) prescribes a breast strike and slightly lifted voice at Nobis quoque peccatoribus; O'Connell governs correct ceremonial practice. [Guéranger PER QUEM](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/) describes a formerly associated blessing of produce; this requires manuscript confirmation and should be displayed, if at all, as 'Guéranger's historical interpretation', not as an established origin.
