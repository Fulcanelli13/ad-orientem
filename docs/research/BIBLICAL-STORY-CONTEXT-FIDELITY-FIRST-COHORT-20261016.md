# Ad Orientem — First Scripture story contextual fidelity review
**16 October 2026 · Research-only · 14 reviewed biblical stories · 0 final excerpt selections**

## What is and is not decided

We are selecting **stories that help readers understand Sacred Scripture and encourage them to explore more**, not filling all 73 Catholic books, manufacturing narrative bridges, or treating liturgical reading boundaries as mandatory lesson boundaries. The existing Ad Orientem Catholic Bible sources and native reader are not replaced.

The primary structured record is `data/explore/biblical-story-context-fidelity-audit.v1.json`. Its 14 records include exact original Atlas/supplementary IDs, source alternatives originally harvested in the 460-row v7 source matrix, proposed coherent passage scopes, specific omission risks, longer context options, OT/NT typological *research* links, modern Catholic chapter URLs, public Douay–Rheims comparison chapter URLs, and a clear HOLD on editorial approval. The companion `data/explore/biblical-story-context-fidelity-review.v1.csv` has reviewer-decision columns.

**Critical Bible witness distinction:** original corpus notes refer to imported **French Crampon and English Challoner** witnesses, with some unresolved numbering exceptions. These 14 contextual readings were checked against an **external Catholic Bible chapter reference** and Douay–Rheims comparison, *not* collated against each imported app file. Do not mark app-verbatim text or French/English verse range equivalence as certified. External USCCB wording is not the app's chosen Scripture translation and has not been copied into the app.

## Evidence-driven conclusions

1. **Genesis 3 (Fall):** the short lectionary 3:9–15 begins after the transgression, so it cannot itself tell the Fall. Preserve temptation, judgment/Protoevangelium and loss of Eden as one comprehensible story.
2. **Genesis 22 (Isaac):** the Catholic published SHORT 22:1–2, 9a, 10–13, 15–18 is a legitimate lectionary selection but cuts Isaac's wood-bearing/question (22:6–8). Those details matter for an accessible narrative and traditional Calvary typology; do not automatically use the shorter cut.
3. **Genesis 45 (Joseph):** 45:1–15 is the missing recognition/reconciliation after betrayal and Egyptian promotion. Genesis 44 Judah's plea is useful longer context. An independent Revised Common Lectionary selection chooses exactly Genesis 45:1–15, but that witness is not retroactively counted as a vote for another Atlas event.
4. **Exodus 12 (Passover):** 12:1–8, 11–14 is a compact liturgical explanation of the lamb and blood, not the full *event*. Narrative needs the outcome 12:29–31 and departure 12:31–42; linked units may be more faithful than a single abridged text.
5. **Luke 1 (Annunciation and Visitation):** 1:26–38 is already self-contained. The Visitation short 1:39–45 stops before the Magnificat; keep 1:46–55 in the Marian story as an internal prayer movement.
6. **John 6 (Bread of Life):** Five separate weekday lectionary cuts are *one family*, not five independent editorial endorsements. No individual slice contains crowd seeking bread, central Eucharistic teaching 6:51–59 *and* the difficult reaction with Peter's confession 6:60–71. Prefer navigable movements of one coherent teaching story.
7. **John 11 (Lazarus):** published shorter composite makes major narrative jumps; 1–44 is a strong first complete story, and 45–53 is valuable follow-on context explaining why the sign leads towards the Passion.
8. **Luke 22 / John 19 (Eucharist and Calvary):** Last Supper 22:14–20 may be concise on its own but 7–13 establishes Passover setting. Calvary 19:17–30 needs the Mother at the Cross 25–27 and Christ's death 28–30; the pierced side 31–37 is a strong linked continuation.
9. **John 20 (Magdalene):** Easter Sunday 20:1–9 does not narrate Mary's personal encounter with Christ. The distinct published 20:1–2, 11–18 reading does. Passage title must match passage substance.
10. **Acts 2 (Pentecost):** festival 2:1–11 shows the Spirit's descent but not Peter's interpretation 14–36 or baptismal response 37–41. Use three navigable movements without creating three obligatory lessons.
11. **Revelation 5 and 21:** starting the Lamb's worship at 5:6 omits the preceding sealed scroll and Lion of Juda (1–5); 5:1–14 is a coherent vision, with published 5:1–10 as one shorter selection. Revelation 21:1–8 (new creation) and 9–27 (symbolic Jerusalem) are separate literary beats, not earthly itinerary stops.
