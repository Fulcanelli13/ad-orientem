## 01. Introit & Preparatory Prayers — `AO.CARD.001`

## 03. Gloria — `AO.CARD.003`

**Existing source-linked manuscript `MM-PRIMARY-MB05` · English**

At the Saviour's birth, the angels proclaim God's glory and the peace brought to mankind (Luke 2:14). The Gloria takes up their acclamation and unfolds it into a hymn of praise: the Church blesses the Father, adores the Son, confesses him as the Lamb who takes away the world's sins, and glorifies the Holy Spirit. Its opening adapts Luke's account; the remainder develops that praise in the Church's own hymn. [Scripture](https://www.newadvent.org/bible/luk002.htm) · [Original 1962 Roman Missal page](https://gregobase.selapa.net/sources/42/300.png).

Le Gloria reprend leur acclamation et la déploie en une grande hymne de louange : l’Église bénit le Père, adore le Fils, le reconnaît comme l’Agneau de Dieu qui enlève les péchés du monde et glorifie le Saint-Esprit. Le début du texte liturgique est adapté du récit de saint Luc ; le reste de l’hymne développe cette louange. ## 08. Sermon / Homily — `AO.CARD.008`

## 11. Orate fratres & Secret — `AO.CARD.011`

## 14. Te igitur — `AO.CANON.01`

**The interpretive problem:** If Christ's sacrifice is complete and infinitely acceptable, why does the priest first ask the Father to accept the offering?

**Source-led explanation (new editorial draft):** The prayer addresses the Father through Christ, placing the sacrifice within the Church's visible communion. Guéranger reads its supplication as ecclesial action even while the celebrant speaks quietly. The mention of the Pope and appropriate bishop expresses communion; it is not merely administrative information added to a private devotion. The question of acceptance will return after the Consecration in Supra quae, but here the Canon is already concerned with the Church's peace, protection and unity.
