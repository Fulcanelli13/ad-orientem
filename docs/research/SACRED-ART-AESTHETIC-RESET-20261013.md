## Curatorial decision order — hard gates, not a weighted score

1. **Is it beautiful and substantial enough?** Real painterly mastery, chromatic richness, figure modelling, pictorial organisation, spiritual gravity. If it is merely historically interesting, classify *contextual* or reject as art. A famous painter's name, museum possession, CC0 license and high JPEG resolution add **zero** artistic merit.
2. **Is it genuinely sacred and devotional?** Religious expression, reverence, visual power; not generic religious iconography, damaged-looking reproduction, decorative flatness, thin amateur genre compositions, bland textbook illustrations, awkward prints or weak studies. Some historical panels/prints may still be kept in a separated documentary collection without being sold as preferred Sacred Art.
3. **Is the subject truthful and appropriate?** The precise Gospel moment, saint and Catholic iconography must be correct. A magnificent related painting is possible only with an accurate subject and relationship label. If no sufficiently good image exists, the theatre scene still exists without artwork.
4. **Only now verify sources and production fitness.** Museum accession, exact original, artist attribution, commercial rights, suitable original reproduction, high resolution, true colour, crop safety and mobile clarity. These protect an aesthetically approved candidate; they cannot *create* it.

There is no weighted arithmetic with 35% beauty plus 65% accuracy/rights that can rescue a mediocre picture. **Beauty is decisive and non-compensatory.**

## Image roles

**Tier A — Exceptional / masterpiece:** truly distinctive high-quality sacred painting; can become a Calendar or Home centerpiece only following the user's artistic selection and technical certification.

**Tier B — Strong devotional or narrative support:** credible traditional painterly image that works well in a Bible passage, saint/devotion or full-image gallery; not claimed as a flagship.

**Tier C — Contextual historical artwork:** altarpiece fragment, print, workshop copy, small detailed panel, Renaissance narrative predella or panoramic textbook art; useful only when it genuinely aids teaching. Its title/source correctness does not promote it into aesthetic recommendations.

**Tier D — Hold / reject / no image:** unsatisfactory artistic quality, unsupported identification or image, prior artist rejection, bad condition/rights, or a scene better handled by text/map. Use no art rather than ruin the coherent visual identity.

### Crucial Old Testament exception

The user relaxed **coverage**, not beauty. For lesser Old Testament passages we should conduct fewer searches and allow maps, timelines, reused quality paintings or no images. It does **not** mean publishing coarser/uglier pictures that dilute the app. Exceptional Old Testament works such as Navarrete's Abraham scene still deserve deep appreciation.
