## III. Source arguments that materially improve the stories

### The altar's comparison with Abel, Abraham and Melchisedech

**What the prayer asks:** *Supra quae* petitions divine favour after the Consecration. The source-critical theological problem is that the Eucharistic offering **infinitely exceeds** ancient sacrifices: on what basis does the priest ask that it be accepted *as* theirs were?

**Reclaimed answer:** Gihr, citing Aquinas III q.83 a.4 ad8, explains that the comparison concerns the *devotion of the human offerers* and God's favourable acceptance, not equal intrinsic dignity of the sacrifices. His discussion separately addresses Abel's faith, Abraham's obedience, and Melchisedech's prefigurative priesthood. [Gihr 1902 pp. 658–659, directly located](https://archive.org/stream/holysacrificeofm00gihriala/holysacrificeofm00gihriala_djvu.txt).

**Cautions:** Gihr's suggestion that fire from heaven consumed Abel's offering is not in Genesis 4. He calls Melchisedech's bread and wine a sacrifice, whereas Genesis 14 records that Melchisedech **brought them forth**; the sacrificial interpretation is traditional typology, not the narrative's literal verb. The Mass lists Abel → Abraham → Melchisedech; biblical history has Abel → Melchisedech → Abraham/Isaac.

**Two different treatments, same evidence:** biblical mode discovers how accepted sacrifice and priesthood are progressively understood; Mass mode confronts the paradox at the precise **post-Consecration prayer**, with longer Guéranger, Gihr and Lanslots original commentary available via Study.

### Avoiding a fictitious theatrical history of Mass gestures

Pierre Le Brun directly disputes making every gesture a literal enactment of a narrated death, calling attention to the inner religious disposition that a movement expresses. He quotes Languet de Gergy in this methodological dispute. [Le Brun, original French, 1843 tome I preface p. xv–xvi](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Lebrun_-_Explication_litt%C3%A9rale_historique_et_dogmatique_des_pri%C3%A8res_et_des_c%C3%A9r%C3%A9monies_de_la_messe_-_Tome_1_(1843).djvu/30).

Therefore, Ferrer's thirty Christ-life correspondences, Dujardin's twenty-two contemplative positions, and the private Passion scheme attributed to Francis de Sales are eligible for the **meditative** path only as *named, voluntary interpretive methods*. The **descriptive** path must not state those are the historical origin of each Roman action. Languet's precise original 1715 words remain uncollated; his doctrinal argument is presently attested via Le Brun's attribution only.

### Hanc igitur and the Day of Atonement

The contemporary Torma article discusses the hands over the offerings and invokes Leviticus 16. [Torma, 26 September 2026](https://tlminayear.substack.com/p/the-eighteenth-sunday-after-pentecost). This is useful evidence that the association is taught in recent lay commentary, not authority on the original 1962 rubric or its historical cause. In Leviticus 16 the **live scapegoat is sent away**, not offered as a slain victim; a claim that it was directly sacrificed requires correction. His *Quam oblationem* description also illustrates the five-cross sequence, but the 1962 Missal/Ritus remains the controlling source.

### Le Brun's structural insight

The [1843 first-volume table of contents](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Lebrun_-_Explication_litt%C3%A9rale_historique_et_dogmatique_des_pri%C3%A8res_et_des_c%C3%A9r%C3%A9monies_de_la_messe_-_Tome_1_(1843).djvu/653) already does something close to our two approaches: start with sacrifice throughout history and its fulfilment in Christ, **then** explain six liturgical divisions of Mass. The book is therefore a high-priority bridge source, although this batch directly read the verified preface and contents, **not all 671 facsimile pages**.

## IV. What we must NOT lose from the research

1. **Ferrer:** the [full thirty-numbered English translation](https://www.svfsermons.org/Parts%20of%20the%20Mass.htm) is directly available; 26 old grouped summaries have been linked back without erasing their relationship to the text. His symbolic model concerns the whole life of Christ including the Ascension, not only Passion episodes. It is not the historical 1962 ceremonial specification.
2. **Dujardin:** the 22 source-mapped headings concern the Mass in the life of Christ, the ecclesiastical year and the history of religion. They are **not authored by St Alphonsus**, though printed in Grimm's edition of his works.
3. **St Alphonsus:** distinguish his own doctrinal and spiritual treatise, his prayer-by-prayer explanation, his historical ceremonial chapters and his editor's supplementary content. The first is especially useful for the biblical arc; the rest substantially enriches Study.
4. **Guéranger:** 30 + 41 French source-anchored contributions are preserved separately and must be integrated by prayer identity, without flattening his contested historical attributions into established facts.
5. **Gihr:** the 1902 [direct OCR full text](https://archive.org/stream/holysacrificeofm00gihriala/holysacrificeofm00gihriala_djvu.txt) is now accessed at specific lines; this is new primary-book evidence, **not recovery of the unlocated earlier 31-claim review**.
6. **Lanslots, Fortescue, Knox, O'Brien, Le Brun, Durand and the Fathers:** their authorial accounts support historical, dogmatic, patristic and allegorical *distinct* explanations; generic prose must not replace those extracts.
7. **Languet and Torma:** the original 1715 book is identified but not chapter-collated; a limited 2026 original Torma article is inspected, with modern copyright and secondary-status limits.
8. **Artwork and Scripture:** borrow the 151-event Bible Atlas' canonical event identities when the exact event is genuinely matched; use one Sacred Art original by artwork ID after curatorial approval. The module does not create a second Bible or image catalogue.
