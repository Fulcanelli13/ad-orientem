### Important exclusion

The National Gallery of Art's [*Christ at the Sea of Galilee* (41637)](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/41637-christ-sea-galilee) formerly had Matthew 14 identifications, but the [Gallery's own scholarly catalogue](https://www.nga.gov/research/publications/italian-paintings-sixteenth-century/italian-paintings-sixteenth-century-christ-sea-galilee-c-1570s) explains that it depicts the **John 21 miraculous catch**: the shore-side Christ, seven disciples, rightward fishing net, sunrise, Peter departing the boat. **Never label this original as Walking on Water, nor silently shift it into Luke 5; John 21 and Luke 5 miraculous catches are separate events.** Retain as a new research possibility for John 21:1–13 only if the corresponding biblical event needs imagery.

## Decision model by priority

**High-value NT:** source-identify and compare 1–3 substantial artistically strong exact works where already known. Engravings can be historical article illustrations, but do not count them as oil-painting masterpieces. If no sufficiently strong image exists, stop rather than pad the page.

**Supporting NT and 29 light OT:** use acquired/researched art when credible; prefer Scripture text, regional route maps, historical/geographical connections, timeline/genealogy, or no visual. No new independent painting search. This does **not** permit false captions or generic decorative saints.

**14 OT anchors:** compare existing leads first; optionally seek a few beautiful representative paintings with accurate scene labels. One original may cover a whole sequence of related narratives only if relation labels distinguish exact event from context.

**Revelation:** never literalize apocalyptic symbolic visions as geographical maps. A few remarkable historical paintings may be appropriate; none compulsory.
