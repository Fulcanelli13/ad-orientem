# Biblical Atlas — three Catholic-edition verse-division adjudications
**12 October 2026 | Research evidence for #1018, not permission to rewrite Scripture witness arrays**

Scope: the three exceptions exposed in Atlas convergence #1069 during the audit of its 151 event passages against the imported Crampon 1923 and Douay–Rheims/Challoner chapter arrays. The findings below compare *edition-labeled published transcriptions*, not certified page-by-page examination of original printed volumes. These are 3 bounded problems, not proof that every remaining witness is fully collated.

## 1. Mark 4:35–41 — terminal verse must not disappear

* 1923 Crampon's published French text explicitly prints Mark **4:41** as the disciples' fearful reaction to Christ's calming of the storm. Sources: [1923 Crampon, complete Gospel of Mark, with publisher/edition provenance](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Marc) and [Crampon Mark 4 online edition](https://www.bibliacatolica.com.br/la-sainte-bible-augustin-crampon-1923/marc/4/).
* The Atlas audit found that the **imported** chapter arrays for both its Crampon and Challoner witnesses lack a standalone slot 4:41 (the terminal words are grouped into 4:40). This **cannot** be presented as an authentic absence in Crampon or as a missing Gospel verse; it is at least a transcription/segmentation problem in the app's witness import.
* Editorial decision: preserve the canonical event reference **Mark 4:35–41**; keep source-warning visible until direct original-volume check and a dedicated provenance-preserving witness import correction. Do not truncate the episode to v.40 merely to silence the test.

## 2. Acts 7:54–60 — Crampon and original Douay reach 60; Challoner reaches 59

* [Crampon 1923 Acts 7](https://www.bibliacatolica.com.br/la-sainte-bible-augustin-crampon-1923/actes-des-apotres/7/) clearly separates Stephen's invocation in **7:59** from his kneeling final prayer in **7:60**.
* The [original pre-Challoner Douay–Rheims transcription of Acts 7](https://originaldouayrheims.com/acts7) also labels the final prayer **7:60**, illustrating why “Douay–Rheims” without naming the revision is not an adequate edition identifier.
* The [Challoner edition printed by Project Gutenberg, book 51](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8351/pg8351-images.html), and [Challoner's Acts text in Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(Douay-Rheims,_Challoner)/Acts) end the chapter at **7:59**; the last prayer is inside v.59.
* Editorial decision: the event **Acts 7:54–60** is a valid 60-verse-system reference when labeled; its Challoner range ends **7:59**. A reader link must be resolved *per edition*, or sent to the unambiguously named full chapter with a printed verse-division warning. Never fabricate a “Challoner 7:60” or imply the final prayer was omitted.

## 3. Exodus 40:1–38 — same ending, different numerical breaks

* [1923 Crampon Exodus, original-book transcription with 1923 Desclée source credit](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Exode): the cloud fills the sanctuary at **40:34–35**, and the guidance of the cloud and fire is separated as **40:36–38**.
* [Challoner's Exodus, book 2, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8302/pg8302-images.html): corresponding closing material is numbered **40:32–36**; **40:36** contains the cloud-by-day/fire-by-night conclusion. The two chapters describe corresponding concluding episodes; numeric verse equality does not imply identical sentence content.
* Editorial decision: maintain **Exodus 40:1–38** as the Crampon-side chapter range, but use **40:1–36** as the Challoner chapter range. Do not invent verses 37 or 38 in Challoner; do not truncate the Crampon passage to 36.
