## Cross-module object reuse and editorial safeguards

- **Rubens, Prado Apostle series:** seven named apostle subjects use seven different painted panels from a coherent *Apostolado* series. These are devotional iconographic identities, not authenticated likenesses.
- **Juan Ribalta, Prado P002965:** **one painting shows Saint Mark and Saint Luke together**, and this **one canonical artwork ID** serves two subject requirements. It is a paired predella panel, not two individual portraits. [Primary museum object](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/saint-mark-and-saint-luke/074cca51-adc7-4a3f-9906-a66c933f12c5).
- **Saint Agnes:** Francisco Rizi's 1665 Baroque oil is labelled P007637 at the Prado. Its expressive brushwork is a promising stylistic direction, but the narrow former retable panel needs composition review.
- **Saint Anselm:** the Louvre's Crespi INV 259 identifies Anselm alongside Martin in a Marian composition. This is **contextual presence, not a solo saint portrait**.
- **Saint Lawrence of Brindisi:** the Italian national register confirms Banchero oil painting 0700031704, but identification is not proof of masterpiece calibre.
- **Saint Joseph:** a single Georges de La Tour painting ID is proposed across his Devotion and Calendar subjects, not duplicated.
- **Saint Teresa/Holy Souls:** one Met Rubens workshop original `met-437542` serves both subjects, with explicit context: Teresa intercedes for souls; do not infer a dedicated portrait.
- **All Saints:** Rubens's *All Saints* at Boijmans is also a single source for the Calendar/Home and Devotion target, not an additional catalogue. Fra Angelico is blocked from the artistic shortlist.
- **Saint Raphael:** the Rembrandt Louvre INV 1736 painting depicts Tobias/Raphael at the end of the journey (Tobit 12), not the earlier fish incident (Tobit 6).
- **Eucharistic Adoration:** Ingres's Virgin adoring the Host is direct Host adoration; Rubens's *Glorification of the Eucharist* is theological allegory, not a monstrance scene.
- **Our Lady of Perpetual Help:** the original icon's Byzantine style conflicts with the aesthetic restriction; no silent exception or fake substitute. This exact subject requires a separate artistic exception decision.
- **Modern saints:** maintain likeness and provenance validation for Saint Thérèse and other photographed-era persons; archival devotional art can be newly researched but not called an authenticated historical portrait.
- **Unconvincing legacy choices:** anonymous formulaic portraits and merely partial group depictions are not automatically accepted. Artistic quality outranks quota completion.
