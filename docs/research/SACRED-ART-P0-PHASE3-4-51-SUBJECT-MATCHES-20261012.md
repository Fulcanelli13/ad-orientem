## Cross-module reuse and safety

- **One Rubens apostolate**: the Prado [*Apostolado del duque de Lerma*](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/saint-john-the-evangelist/29bfd358-bfd3-43b4-a894-b57f6eb2d81f) yields seven individually documented paintings. Do not create a second painting per apostle or present devotional portraits as proven contemporary likenesses.
- **Joseph**: Georges de La Tour's Louvre painting is the *same canonical proposed object* for the Devotion of Saint Joseph and Calendar Saint Joseph. Do not duplicate its eventual image asset.
- **Teresa / Purgatory**: Met `met-437542` is the same Rubens workshop object for Teresa and Holy Souls. Teresa is depicted in an intercession scene; this is not a dedicated historical portrait.
- **All Saints**: Rubens's Boijmans *All Saints* is also a **single** source for the Calendar/homepage All Saints research candidate and the Devotion. The rejected Fra Angelico alternatives remain historically recorded but are not to be shown again.
- **Raphael**: Rembrandt's 1637 Louvre painting depicts the archangel leaving Tobias after events of **Tobit 12**. It does not depict the earlier fish incident in Tobit 6. It may serve a saint contextual illustration, not an unrelated Gospel.
- **Direct Eucharistic adoration**: Ingres's *Virgin Adoring the Host* actually depicts the Host. Rubens's *Glorification of the Eucharist* is a theological allegory, not literal monstrance exposition. These remain distinct alternatives.
- **Original icon exception**: Our Lady of Perpetual Help has a canonical original Byzantine-style icon. Do not silently override the general no-flat-style instruction or choose a false Baroque reproduction; this specific conflict needs an explicit artistic decision.
- **Weak-lead filter**: the pre-existing anonymous Saint Cyril of Alexandria portrait is officially described as relatively stereotyped, Saint Cyril of Jerusalem is documented as a fresco rather than an oil panel, and the monumental Vallejo Immaculate Conception is primarily a multi-figure Marian tableau in which Canisius is only one figure. These are not automatically promoted to highest-quality saint illustrations.
