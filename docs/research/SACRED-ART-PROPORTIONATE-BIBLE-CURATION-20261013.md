# Sacred Art — proportionate biblical curation policy (13 October 2026)

**Editorial research policy, not a change to human-selected artwork.** The Bible Theatre's narrative, Scripture, genealogy, geography and timeline coverage is not conditional on an illustration. A blank image slot is valid when the right historical painting does not exist, is weak, misidentified, or adds nothing.

## Guiding change

**Prior error:** treating 83 outstanding Bible art-subject records as 83 obligatory unique masterpiece hunts. A "research" status in the 235-subject user-choice ledger means an artistic choice is undecided, **not** that a new image must exist for every narrative scene. Even the 111 initial Bible artwork slots are editorial *opportunities*, not proof that 111 images are needed. One artwork may support many narrative entries with precise distinctions.

**New policy:** emphasize New Testament curation; relax Old Testament per-event demands, not truth or reproduction standards. Research a handful of aesthetically exceptional OT anchors and reuse them where iconographically warranted. In lesser scenes a map, route, schematic, genealogical diagram, timeline, or no picture is often superior. Do not automatically downgrade the aesthetic standard merely because an artwork depicts the Old Testament: use quality when shown, spend much less research effort where art does not repay it.

The **40 NT subjects** retain an appropriate 30/10 deep-versus-selective source-investigation split. **These are research effort categories, not mandatory picture quotas.**

### Specific cases

- **Genesis 3:** an Expulsion painting describes the aftermath, not the exact temptation; suitable only with an accurate subevent label.
- **Noah:** a credible Flood painting may support the wider Noah narrative; the Covenant need not receive an independent second work.
- **Calling of Abram; travel to Egypt; Joshua at Jordan; return from exile:** geography, paths, maps and timelines are the principal illustration.
- **Tabernacle and Temple reconstruction:** a sourced diagram or architectural model may be more useful than an anachronistic painting.
- **Revelation:** art may visualize theological symbolism, but not as literal historical topography.
- **Emmaus:** paintings of the *Supper* illustrate the recognition at table, not the initial road encounter.
- **John 21:** *Christ Feeds Peter’s Sheep* and *Peter Commissioned by Risen Christ* may share an exact canonical painting and event reference.
- **Acts travel:** chronology and route maps are often preferable to invented local event paintings.

## Selection protocol (same truth and source gates in every lane)

1. **Already acquired:** review relevant originals from the canonical 266-SHA archive; compare exact artist/version, original object's subject, condition and image quality. Do not prefer a weak painting merely because it is available.
2. **Previously researched, not acquired:** use existing museum-led source options before starting new internet research. Verify exact accession and attributed artist. Reconcile 101 earlier user choices and older legacy approvals without overwriting them.
3. **Fresh internet discovery:** required only for high-value NT narratives, 14 major OT anchor opportunities, or another scene where an evidently great composition is actually available. Prefer custodian catalogues, source editions and institutionally verified reproductions over social or search thumbnails. Record source URL, accession, attribution, date, subject evidence, rights and the precise scripture event.
4. **Comparative aesthetics:** favor solemn, beautiful, high-quality Renaissance/Baroque/naturalistic sacred art. Preserve hard artist/style holds, including El Greco and Fra Angelico for new Ad Orientem selections. However absence of such a painting is a reason for no-image, not to mislabel a mediocre one.
5. **Editorial resolution:** a subject's art outcome is `PAINTING_CURATED`, `PAINTING_REUSED_WITH_ACCURATE_RELATION`, `MAP_OR_SCHEMATIC`, `TEXT_TIMELINE_GENEALOGY_ONLY`, or `NO_IMAGE_NEEDED`. The narrative is complete in every case, irrespective of artwork.
6. **Release:** still require a verified exact master, precise attribution, global commercial rights, SHA-256, no frame/glare, composition-friendly phone rendering and the human's artistic acceptance. No research record is by itself publication permission.

## What this changes—and does not change

- The previous 83 "research" slots still exist as a subject/asset-opportunity inventory, **not** 83 forced art gaps.
- This does **not** change the 28 Bible already selected, nor the overall 101 choices, 235 catalogued subjects, the archive's hashes, or any production assets.
- New thematic effort should go to the life of Christ, Our Lady, Apostles, Gospel miracles/parables and pivotal Acts; traditional Catholic sources and strong historic iconography matter more than exhaustive painting-per-verse completion.
- The process still respects the five stages of requirements → inventory → matching → genuine gaps → superior reproductions. Crucially, stage 4 *can conclude that a painting is not needed*.
- This policy is isolated to research and must not overwrite current Bible Theatre canonical event identifiers or another pipeline's runtime work.
