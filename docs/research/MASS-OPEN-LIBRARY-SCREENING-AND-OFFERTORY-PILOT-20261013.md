# Roman Mass × Bible — open-library screening and first comparative-reading pilot
13 October 2026 · Research-only on Mass–Bible PR #1082. No changes to 1962 Mass runtime, source selection, rubrics, Biblical Atlas, Sacred Art or public flags.

## Method: discover -> filter -> access-test -> read -> compare

The aim is **not** to collect every Catholic liturgy book. Include an edition in detailed research only if (1) it concerns the Roman Mass or a specifically necessary scriptural, theological or historical question; (2) the exact work/edition is identifiable; (3) its relevant text is **actually open** as complete HTML/OCR or a downloadable original (the latter still requiring scan verification); and (4) it adds evidence not supplied by a better/earlier witness. Rights-to-read and rights-to-redistribute are separate.

### Bibliographical safeguards

- [O'Connell OCR](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/d-The%20Celebration%20of%20Mass-%20O%27Connel%201962_OCR.txt) is the **fourth revised edition incorporating the 1962 Missal**, whose revision preface is dated 1963 and printing/copyright 1964. A filename with “1962” does *not* establish imprint date.
- The [Saint Libère Le Brun](https://www.liberius.net/livre.php?id_livre=388) download offered is an **1860 reprint**. The individually accessible [Wikisource scan](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Lebrun_-_Explication_litt%C3%A9rale_historique_et_dogmatique_des_pri%C3%A8res_et_des_c%C3%A9r%C3%A9monies_de_la_messe_-_Tome_1_(1843).djvu) is **1843**. BnF indexes the original four-volume publication as **1716–1726**. Never combine page numbers between editions.
- Beaulac's readable PDF **search excerpt** says his book draws on Gihr, Croegaert, Grimaud, Dom Gaspard Lefebvre and Vandeur; these are *candidate bibliographic leads*, not five books automatically selected or fully read.
- The “red Missal” remains unassigned to a particular imprint; do not guess Campion. Modern Ratzinger/Hahn are only used on a case-specific theological question and when lawful access to the relevant text exists.

## First direct multi-author source comparison — *In spiritu humilitatis* at the Offertory

This is an **additional candidate distinct from the existing 26 MB01–MB26 examples**. Do not silently rename it MB27, alter arrays or promote publication until section-owner review.

**A. Authoritative ritual witness (OCR textual evidence, not scan):**
[Missale Romanum 1962 OCR](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt), *Ritus servandus* VII,5 (OCR around lines 10571–10586). After the chalice offering, the priest covers the chalice, places joined hands on the altar, inclines moderately and says the prayer **secretly**. The same OCR has prior water/wine mixing in VII,4. This is an OCR rubric witness; do not declare the original printed page inspected.

**B. Contemporary ceremonial explanation:** 
[O'Connell full OCR](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/d-The%20Celebration%20of%20Mass-%20O%27Connel%201962_OCR.txt), printed **pp. 252–253** under “In Spiritu Humilitatis,” OCR lines ~12528–12530; also earlier quotation of VII,5 at ~12471–12482. Confirms joined hands resting on altar, bow and silent recitation as prescribed, separate from the following *Veni sanctificator*. This book is not the historical origin of the prayer.

**C. Primary Catholic Scripture:**
[Clementine Daniel 3:38–40](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Vulgata_Clementina/Prophetia_Danielis) has Azariah's prayer in the furnace: sacrificial worship is absent; he asks that a contrite and humble spirit be accepted in lieu of the sacrifices. The Vulgate clause **“in animo contrito, et spiritu humilitatis suscipiamur”** is reordered/adapted in the liturgical prayer; verse 40 gives **“sic fiat sacrificium nostrum in conspectu tuo hodie, ut placeat tibi”**. Label the relationship **SCRIPTURAL_WORDING_ADAPTED_FROM_DANIEL_3_39_40** rather than a word-for-word unchanged citation.
[Crampon Daniel 3:24–40](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Daniel) explicitly identifies Azarias as the speaker in verse 25 and reads verse 39 in French as the penitential plea; compare vv. 38–40. Note that this passage belongs to Catholic Daniel's Greek additions and the numbering of other editions may vary. Do not exclude it because an abbreviated Protestant Daniel omits it.

**D. Traditional French authorial exposition:**
[Guéranger 1885, paragraph under Offertoire](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/) gives the Latin Mass prayer and calls it the words of the three children in the furnace, citing Daniel 3:39–40. He treats it as the concluding humble petition of the offertory prayers. The passage does not claim the 1962 rubrics already existed in Daniel.

**Draft explanation for later author review (NOT currently published):**
> À l'Offertoire, après avoir offert le calice, le prêtre s'incline et demande que Dieu reçoive le sacrifice présenté avec un esprit d'humilité et un cœur contrit. La prière reprend, en l'adaptant, Daniel 3,39–40 : dans la fournaise, Azarias reconnaît l'absence des sacrifices du Temple et implore l'accueil d'une offrande intérieure. Dans la Messe, ces paroles disposent le célébrant à offrir humblement le sacrifice eucharistique ; elles ne signifient pas que la Messe reproduit l'épisode de la fournaise.

## Critical author conflict to track, not smooth away

In the same Guéranger 1885 [Offertory section](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/) the notes assert sweeping historical unchangeability of the Canon. The [1929 DTC liturgy article](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_de_th%C3%A9ologie_catholique/MESSE_VIII._La_messe_dans_la_liturgie_7._Le_De_sacramentis_du_Pseudo_Ambroise) instead documents ancient differences in prayer ordering/text relative to later Roman Canon. Preserve Guéranger's devotional exposition where relevant; **do not publish universal historical invariance as fact** without manuscript criticism. This demonstrates why original author texts must be compared rather than concatenated.
