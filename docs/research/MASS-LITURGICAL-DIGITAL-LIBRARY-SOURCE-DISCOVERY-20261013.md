# Roman Mass × Bible — open digital-library discovery, initial catalogue pass

**13 October 2026 · Research discovery only · Existing Mass–Bible PR #1082 · No publication, textual certification or app changes.**

## Corrected sequence

1. Survey the **collections/catalogues** of digitised older Catholic liturgical literature, especially French and Latin. Search across titles and subjects; do not begin with a fixed list of authors.
2. Retain works directly useful for **the traditional Roman Mass**: exact texts/rubrics, ceremonial practice, history of particular Mass prayers, biblical roots, sound theological explanation, French catechesis or Liturgical Year Propers. Do not equate a generic book about Catholicism, church politics or the Breviary with a Mass research source.
3. Establish **actual access mode and edition** before detailed reading: DIRECT_SEARCHABLE_TEXT (full publicly accessible OCR/HTML), CATALOG_LISTS_PDF (linked full scan offered; image/text not yet inspected), or CATALOG_ONLY (bibliography/holding, no open file identified). Record edition year separately from book's treatment of a 1962 rite.
4. Build a **small, cross-functional full-text working corpus** from the most useful accessible works. Only afterwards examine passages by Mass section with page, exact quotation and Scripture anchors, keeping original rubrics separate from historical claims, typologies and optional meditations.
5. Open accessibility does not automatically authorize **commercial redistribution**, reuse of OCR, scan rights, or posthumous editions. Verify rights before copying into the app; linking to sources is different from importing their pages.

## Deliberately not first-wave

- **Breviary-only material, generic Church history, Gregorian music-only works**: legitimate Catholic books, but not central to the requested Mass–Scripture explanation unless an exact question calls for them.
- **Ratzinger, Scott Hahn and other relatively modern copyrighted authors**: useful selective interpretative witnesses; not part of the older freely reusable digital-book core unless an open lawful source for the exact passage is established.
- **The “red missal”**: exact edition/imprint has not been established. Do not silently conflate it with Campion or any other edition.
- **Restricted, catalog-only holdings**: bibliographic leads only, not claimed close-read.

## Important discoveries and corrections

1. **O'Connell is already searchable in a complete 30,000+ line transcription**, including his source bibliography and the explicit distinction between binding rubrics and rubricists' opinions. The 1962-year label on the archive file is misleading as a publication date.
2. **The 1962 Missal has a separate complete OCR text in the same Isidore collection** (100,000+ OCR lines) and a smaller 29 MB PDF listed. This offers a new route around the earlier 82 MB PDF retrieval blockage, but **OCR is not printed-page certification**, especially for tables, accents and two-column layouts.
3. **French works previously treated as missing from the corpus are discoverable as complete PDF offerings**, including Le Brun and Gihr; stop declaring their content independently read until pages are examined.
4. **Beaulac (1941)** is a promising French practical instructional text. Its preface explains that it draws on Gihr, Croegaert, Grimaud, Lefebvre and Vandeur; those are **leads**, not five automatically required new sources.
