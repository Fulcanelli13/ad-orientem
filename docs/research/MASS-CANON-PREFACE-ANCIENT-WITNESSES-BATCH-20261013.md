## High-value source discrimination: the archangel at the incense altar

**Observed 1962-rite wording:** the Offertory blessing of incense names Saint Michael and describes him as standing at the right of the incense altar. It is NOT a Gospel quotation as a whole, and its use is tied to Mass with incensation. Cross-check the exact scan in the 1962 edition before publication.

**Luke 1:11 and 1:19:** in the story of the priest Zachary, the angel standing at the right of the incense altar identifies himself as Gabriel. Crampon renders the two details separately and clearly. This is ONE biblical event (annunciation of St John the Baptist) and should not be reclassified as an appearance of St Michael.

**Revelation 8:3–4:** the apocalyptic heavenly liturgy depicts another angel offering incense and the saints' prayers at the heavenly altar; this angel is NOT identified by name in those verses. Do not merge that scene, Zachary's temple vision, and the Offertory prayer into one event or pretend that Revelation explicitly names Michael.

**Gihr p.189:** discusses that several older missals employed Gabriel and defends the received Michael invocation as tradition. His interpretation and historical manuscript claims must be identified as his argument and checked against editions before asserted as critical liturgical history.

## Critical early witness: Received Roman Canon is not literal De sacramentis

Read the Latin De sacramentis IV.5.21–23, IV.6.26–27 and V.1.1–4. The witness contains consecratory prayer language, an anamnesis of Passion/Resurrection/Ascension, the three biblical sacrifices, and a petition that offerings be accepted on the heavenly altar. At IV.6.27 the early formula puts the heavenly altar petition before the three patriarchal offerings and uses plural 'angelorum tuorum'; the received Roman Canon puts Supra quae before Supplices and uses singular 'sancti Angeli tui'. This is *textual evidence*, not a claim that the text represents the entire celebration, an ancient 1962 Ordo, or an incontestably Ambrosian autograph. The DTC describes De sacramentis as attributed to Ambrose/Pseudo-Ambrose and discusses differing textual orders.

On Melchisedech, Genesis 14 itself describes his bringing forth bread and wine and his priestly status, but does not narrate the actual act of offering those elements as a sacrifice in explicit words. The ancient Christian sacrificial reading is supplied by De sacramentis and Cyprian; the Roman Canon explicitly describes his oblation. Keep three evidential levels distinct.

Thomas Aquinas III q.83 a.4 ad 9 further guards the heavenly-altar explanation: the prayer does NOT ask that the Eucharistic species or Christ's physical body be borne literally into heaven. Thomas uses mystical-body/prayers/ecclesial/Christological interpretations, connecting to the incense of Revelation 8. This prohibits a visualisation in which an angel physically removes the Eucharist from the altar.

## Concise French explanations for later author review — not published

### L'ange de l'encensement

« À l'Offertoire des messes où l'on emploie l'encens, l'Église invoque saint Michel archange. La prière évoque l'autel de l'encens, mais il faut distinguer cette invocation liturgique de deux passages bibliques : saint Luc raconte qu'un ange se tenait à droite de l'autel de l'encens lors de l'annonce à Zacharie, et cet ange se nomme Gabriel (Lc 1,11.19) ; l'Apocalypse décrit un ange qui offre l'encens avec les prières des saints (Ap 8,3-4), sans lui donner de nom. Il s'agit de rapprochements scripturaires et liturgiques, non de trois récits de saint Michel. »

### Le sacrifice des anciens justes dans le Canon

« Dans le Canon romain, le prêtre rappelle Abel, Abraham et Melchisédech. Ces trois noms renvoient à des récits distincts de la Genèse, repris dans l'interprétation chrétienne de l'unique sacrifice du Christ. Une ancienne prière eucharistique transmise dans le De sacramentis les associe déjà à l'autel céleste, mais dans un ordre et avec des mots qui ne coïncident pas entièrement avec le Canon reçu. Le lien est réel et ancien ; l'identité mot à mot ne l'est pas. »

### L'autel céleste

« L'imploration du Canon adressée à Dieu au sujet de l'autel céleste ne signifie pas qu'un ange transporterait matériellement les espèces consacrées hors de l'autel. Saint Thomas explique la prière en rapport avec l'union de l'Église et du Christ et la présentation des prières à Dieu, rapprochant cette image de l'Apocalypse. »
