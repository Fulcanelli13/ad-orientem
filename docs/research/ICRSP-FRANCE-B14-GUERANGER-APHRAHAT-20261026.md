# ICRSP France — B14: Guéranger passage comparison and Aphrahat's separate Elijah/Elisha witnesses

**26 October 2026** · Issue #1665 · [draft research PR #1666](https://github.com/Fulcanelli13/ad-orientem/pull/1666) · No changes to production Scripture, Calendar, Mass Explorer, Mass reader, icons, postures, music or liturgical Proper.

## Substantive result

**Four** ICRSP day commentaries were compared at their exact existing 1962 source paths: Naaman (Mon. Lent III), Susanna (Sat. Lent III), Elisha/Nain (Thu. Lent IV) and Elijah/Lazarus (Fri. Lent IV). **Six** existing B12 claim IDs are covered. Two Guéranger passages (Naaman and Susanna) have been located, with parallel passage wording, in a second online host of his French work. **Three** more original-author section loci in Aphrahat's *Demonstrations* are directly located in the historical English translation (VIII.11, XXI.14, XXI.15), and the precise relation class is preserved. This is source-claim research, **not** complete original-printed-edition or original-Syriac critical collation.

### Guéranger: exact day and section alignments, but no original print scans

The [Guéranger association's Carême transcription](https://www.domgueranger.net/lannee-liturgique-le-careme-propre-du-temps/?lang=en) includes the actual text of both source sections. Its companion title-page transcription explicitly identifies *Le Carême*, **16e édition, H. Oudin, 1903**. The diocesan catalogue also lists a physically held **1903, 639-page** *Le Carême*. The matching hosted text **cannot automatically be called an independent 1903 print verification**; ICRSP and the association may depend on the same transcription.

- **Monday Lent III, Naaman:** compare [ICRSP `a0191_ancien`](https://icrspfrance.fr/textes-liturgiques.php?article=a0191_ancien), *Dom Guéranger / LEÇON* with the association's **LE LUNDI DE LA TROISIÈME SEMAINE DE CARÊME / LEÇON**. Both have the specific, post-lesson teaching that Naaman's leprosy signifies sin, Jordan immersion anticipates Baptism, and divine grace transforms through humble means. Luke 4:23–30's **direct** mention of Naaman is a separately typed biblical cross-reference. Ambrose *De mysteriis* III.16–17 (B13) independently attests the sacramental interpretation. Do not construe this as Ambrose being explicitly cited by Guéranger.
- **Saturday Lent III, Susanna:** compare [ICRSP `a0198_ancien`](https://icrspfrance.fr/textes-liturgiques.php?article=a0198_ancien), *Dom Guéranger / LEÇON* with association's **LE SAMEDI DE LA TROISIÈME SEMAINE DE CARÊME / LEÇON**. The author distinguishes Susanna's deserved vindication from the mercy extended to the woman in John 8; then interprets Susanna as the faithful Church. The **biblical lesson is disjoint Daniel 13**, not the entire chapter. The claim of an early Christian sarcophagus/catacomb visual tradition is still **not** individual-object/archaeology verified.

**A useful historical citation has been retrieved instead of inventing one:** the association transcription preserves Guéranger's footnote **56**, which reads `In Danielem, pag. 27. Edit. Fabricii.` The relevant edition family is Johann Albert Fabricius's *S. Hippolyti episcopi et martyris opera*, Hamburg **1716–1718** ([Catholic Encyclopedia overview](https://www.catholic.com/encyclopedia/hippolytus-saints)). This is **a direct locator from Guéranger's authorial footnote**, but the actual **Fabricius page 27 has not been visually inspected**. It is **NOT** the same as p. 25 of the modern, ©2010 T. C. Schmidt translation located in B13. Do not conflate those print editions or reproduce the modern copyrighted translation.

**A misleading candidate scan avoided:** the [Internet Archive item `lanneliturgiqu05gu`](https://archive.org/details/lanneliturgiqu05gu), linked by the Guéranger site, explicitly lists **publication year 1911**, volume 5. The scan is *not* the 1903 edition, and the PDF itself was not opened for visual/page verification in this batch. Consequently, **0/2** Guéranger day paragraphs have 1903 print-page identity checked.

### Aphrahat: two different resurrection and Christological relationships

**Textual and bibliographic basis:** Aphrahat is a fourth-century Syriac author. The [Syri.ac Aphrahat source index](https://syri.ac/aphrahat) identifies J. Parisot's *Aphraatis Sapientis Persae Demonstrationes*, vol. 1 (1894, Syriac with Latin), and the 1898 NPNF selected English translations. **Translator attribution correction:** the NPNF volume's [editorial preface](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf213.iii.ii.html) credits **Albert Edward Johnston** with translating the Aphrahat selections and **John Gwynn** with editorial revision. Calling Gwynn the translator would be inaccurate.

1. [*Demonstration VIII*, §11](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/370108.htm) discusses **Elijah's** widow-son restoration (*1 Kings 17*) and **Elisha's** Shunammite child restoration (*2 Kings 4*) as distinct evidence that God raises the dead. **Evidence type:** *general resurrection testimony*, not an exact Gospel quotation or prediction.
2. [*Demonstration XXI*, §14](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/370121.htm) expressly contrasts Elijah and Christ: Elijah raises the widow's son, while Christ restores the widow's son, Lazarus and the ruler's daughter. **Evidence type:** *explicit early Christian Elijah–Christ analogy*. Even here, Aphrahat does **not** explicitly state that *1 Kings 17* directly predicts or textually quotes *John 11*.
3. [*Demonstration XXI*, §15](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/370121.htm) offers other Elisha–Christ correspondences and refers to a dead man revived by contact with Elisha's **bones**, which is **2 Kings 13:20–21**, **not** the Shunammite child in *2 Kings 4:25–38*. **Evidence type:** *explicit Elisha–Christ analogy via a different Old Testament event*. No claim that Aphrahat attests the exact Elisha/Nain liturgical pair.

**Cross-check with actual Guéranger day prose:** [ICRSP Thu. Lent IV](https://icrspfrance.fr/textes-liturgiques.php?article=a0205_ancien) independently uses Elisha as a figure of Christ before the Luke 7 Nain Gospel; [ICRSP Fri. Lent IV](https://icrspfrance.fr/textes-liturgiques.php?article=a0206_ancien) compares Elijah's compassion to the Son of God before John 11 Lazarus. Thus both have strong **nineteenth-century interpretive witnesses** and partial/related **fourth-century patristic support**; do not incorrectly label Aphrahat as directly commenting on the exact 1962 pairing.
