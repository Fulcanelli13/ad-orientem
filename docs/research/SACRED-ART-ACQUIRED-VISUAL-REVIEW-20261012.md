# Sacred Art — evaluation of already acquired original paintings

**12 October 2026; branch `platform/885-sacred-art-candidates`; PR #887 remains draft.**

This batch is the first disciplined actual-composition review of the canonical **50 already-acquired paintings** linked to 27 of the 134 unsatisfied subjects. It does **not** start a fresh acquisitions campaign. We matched images to original museum object identities, used institutional source records, kept original hashes, and considered readability, composition, painterly quality, art style and theological specificity. The recorded source URLs lead to the real artworks; a third-party similarly titled painting is *not* a substitute.

## Source and identity corrections applied

- Cleveland collection object 1950.400 is **workshop of Jacopo Tintoretto**, not an autograph painting by Tintoretto. Source: https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1950.400.
- Metropolitan Museum object 436302 is **Workshop of Domenico Fetti**, not autograph Fetti. Source: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/436302.
- Both artist corrections have been applied to the canonical `data/calendar/sacred-art-candidates.v1.json` while preserving all 281 painting objects and existing acquired SHA-256 files.
- Cleveland Gandolfi *The Holy Family* is the 1776 painting (1998.10); similarly titled oval Gandolfi auction art is a **different object** and must not be used for its visual review.
- Juan de Flandes *Marriage Feast at Cana* shows the Cana wedding and water jars, but should be associated with **John 2** and not an unrelated Sunday by topical similarity.
- Velázquez's *Supper at Emmaus* documents the **recognition at supper** (Luke 24:30–31), not the conversation on the road (Luke 24:13–27). Prefer event-substage label when linking to Bible Atlas.
- Saint Lucy comparisons: Zurbarán is the stronger visual priority. Francesco del Cossa has accurate martyr symbols but is a medieval/Renaissance gold-ground alternative.
