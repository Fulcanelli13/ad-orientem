The editorial notice states the little *Sacrifice* treatise originally appeared elsewhere in the complete works and was reproduced here. **The same piece must not be counted as two independent sources simply because it appears in volumes VI and XIII.** St Alphonsus says that he draws on a learned French author; Grimm conjectures a possible relation to **Pierre de Condren's *L'Idée du sacerdoce et du sacrifice de Jésus-Christ*** (as mediated by an Oratorian editor) but explicitly leaves identification of the particular intermediary uncertain. Do not turn that editorial hypothesis into a definitive author source without source comparison.

## Two separate but linked research experiences

### Journey A — *La vie du Christ contemplée dans la Messe*

Begin at the **actual liturgical order**, and use Dujardin's **22 successive meditation headings** to guide the faithful through Scriptural scenes and doctrinal themes. This is a **meditative overlay** rather than a history of ceremonies. In particular, Dujardin's analogy of the Lavabo and the washing of the apostles' feet is **not** evidence that the medieval Roman handwashing originated to commemorate John 13; similarly, the Pax Domini commingling and Resurrection is an author's meditative interpretation rather than its archaeological origin.

Direct section-in-source register: [22 heading-by-heading entries](../../data/geography/research/mass-alphonsus-volume-xiii-two-journeys-20261011.v1.json) `dujardinMeditativeStations`; printed pp. 17–21. All proposed exact Bible chapters there are **editorial research associations**, not exact quotations made by Dujardin; use original paragraphs before assigning verse sources to the author.

Dujardin's narrative really is expansive:
- **Preparation / Foot:** Creation, first man, fall, repentance and the promised Redeemer.
- **Kyrie, Gloria, readings and Gospel:** expectation of Messiah, Nativity, Christ's hidden life, prophetic preparation and Gospel teaching.
- **Offertory, Lavabo, Preface, Canon:** Last Supper, washing of feet, Christ's intercession in the garden, Old Law sacrifices, the Cross and the dead.
- **Lord's Prayer to Communion:** Holy Sepulchre, Resurrection, the Lamb and spiritual Communion.
- **Final prayers:** Ascension, Cenacle, Pentecost and worldwide Gospel mission.

**Editorial cautions to certify:** his assertion of **four thousand years** awaiting the Messiah is devotional chronology, not an established biblical date; his comments that Christ descends into “Limbo and Purgatory” should not be presented as settled dogmatic geography; “Jesus no longer on the altar” after Communion must not imply Christ cannot remain substantially present in any reserved Eucharist. The connection at the elevation must be framed as the one sacrifice made sacramentally present, **not another physical crucifixion**.

**Important third axis: the actual 1962 liturgical calendar.** The preceding list only addresses fixed parts of the Mass. The changing **Propers**—Introit, Gradual, Alleluia/Tract, Offertory, Communion chant, Epistle/Gospel or extra lessons—supply a much broader Scriptural corpus across Sundays, feast days, Ember days, Holy Week and the Easter Vigil. The same Feast, Bible episode, reading and historical context may be mapped through the source-owned **resolved 1962 Proper**, independently of a symbolic explanation of the Roman Ordinary. ## St Alphonsus's own biblical contribution deserves independent treatment

*The Sacrifice of Jesus Christ* has a clear teaching structure that can be used directly as the foundation of an evidence-led “Sacrifice through Scripture” journey:

- **Four purposes** of Old Testament sacrifices: adoration/peace, thanksgiving, expiation, petition. This is **his classificatory theological exposition**, not the claim every Mosaic sacrifice matches one of four static card types.
- **Five sacrificial conditions:** consecration, oblation, immolation, consumption and participation. He argues how these find their fulfilment in the mystery of Christ and the Eucharist; older French theological reasoning and his own conclusions are not identical.
- **Prophetic fulfilment and the new priesthood:** he cites Isaiah 53, Hebrews 10, John 10, Luke 1 and Psalm 109 (Vulgate) to show Christ as consecrated victim and the priest in the order of Melchizedek.
- **Continuity and change:** the one offering on Calvary is not “redone” as a fresh bloody sacrifice; the Sacrifice of the Altar applies the fruit of redemption according to Tridentine teaching.
- **Heavenly consummation:** an earlier French interpretation and Alphonsus's own response disagree on aspects of “consumption” and sacrificial participation; preserve the debate and Grimm's editorial footnote as **three different authorship layers**.

The *Short Explanation of the Prayers of Mass* is not just another dictionary: it actually follows **six Mass sections** from the prayers at the foot of the altar through the thanksgiving after Communion, and explicitly notes the angelic **Luke 2** origin of `Gloria`, the inherited **Psalm 42** `Judica`, water/wine symbolism, `Lavabo`, Roman Canon and Communion. It also makes historical attributions that must be checked independently rather than imported as facts.

The collection of **prayers before/after Mass and Communion** belongs to existing Prayer Library modules. Separate works by Thomas Aquinas, Bonaventure and Francis de Sales appearing within Grimm's compilation must retain their actual authorship, even if printed under a saint's-volume title.

## Editorial and interaction design, after research completion

Canonical data entities:
- `massPrayerAnchor` — an existing stable 1962 prayer/rubric phrase ID; cannot be a temporary “card 48” index.
- `scripturePassage` — exact reference, edition, full internal reader link.
- `sourceInterpretation` — author, work, printed page/section, actual proposition, citation, relationship grade.
- `meditativeJourneyNode` — optional, e.g. Dujardin DJ01..DJ22, with no false historical origin.
- `calendarProperReference` — the current **verified** lesson/Proper in the 1962 text source and date, separate from optional typology.
- `biblicalEpisode/Person/Place` — existing Bible Atlas registry IDs only, never a fabricated pin.
- `artwork` — church-historical reception (e.g. Ravenna mosaic) clearly separate from supposed appearance of biblical events.

UI after certification should have two entry routes:
1. **From Mass:** “Understand the Scriptural sources” and a separate “Contemplate a traditional meditation”; both return to exact original liturgical context. Beginner Guided Mass may optionally consume concise approved notes, but LIVE/clean Missal remain unchanged.
2. **From Atlas:** each Bible episode lists both `quotedInMass` and `prefiguresMass/meditatedDuringMass` as **distinct** categories, with the source author's argument and an exact Mass prayer/Propers link.

Distinct scroll progressions must be maintained: Mass order ≠ Bible chronology ≠ Gospel harmony ≠ liturgical-calendar cycle. Using one timeline as a substitute for all four would lead to a misleading historical composition.
