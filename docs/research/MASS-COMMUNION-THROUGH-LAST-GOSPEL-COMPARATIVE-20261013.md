## Six source-critical conclusions with directly linked witnesses

### 1. Lord's Prayer vs the liturgical embolism

The full prayer comes from the Lord's instruction ([Matthew 6:9–13](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Matthieu); [Luke 11:2–4](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Luc)), while the priest's liturgical preface Praeceptis salutaribus and the Libera nos with appeals for peace and intercession are additional **Roman composition**. The [1962 Ritus X](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt) and [Guéranger, L'Oraison dominicale / Libera nos](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/) are consistent on the liturgical sequence; historical assertions that the Pater has always occupied this exact place in all rites are not proved merely by Guéranger's statement. [O'Connell p.275–276](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/d-The%20Celebration%20of%20Mass-%20O%27Connel%201962_OCR.txt) describes ceremonial handling of the paten and voice.

### 2. Fraction, Emmaus, and commixture cannot be collapsed into one supposed origin

The 1962 [Ritus X.1–2](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt) and [O'Connell pp.277–278](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/d-The%20Celebration%20of%20Mass-%20O%27Connel%201962_OCR.txt) identify the prescribed division of the Host, small fragment, triple sign over chalice, and placing the particle in the Precious Blood. Scripture reports breaking bread at the Last Supper and recognition of Christ at Emmaus ([Luke 24:30–35](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Luc)); [Durandus chapter 51 §IV](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Rational_(Durand_de_Mende)/Volume_2/Quatri%C3%A8me_livre/Chapitre_51) invokes Emmaus as his medieval **symbolic explanation**, not proof that the precise Roman rite began at Emmaus. [Guéranger, Libera nos](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/) proposes that the mingling signifies Christ's resurrected Body and Blood reunited; preserve as Guéranger's spiritual reading rather than uncontested historical or physical mechanism.

### 3. John the Baptist and the centurion — the exact scriptural wording distinction

[John 1:29, Crampon 1923](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Jean) is the actual Baptist's proclamation; the Mass's threefold Agnus Dei **adds petitions**. The 1962 normal formula ends dona nobis pacem, whereas [Requiem Ritus XIII](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt) prescribes dona eis requiem / sempiternam, **no breast strikes**, no first peace prayer and no kiss of peace. [Matthew 8:8](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Matthieu) narrates the centurion's request about a servant; liturgical Domine non sum dignus adapts sanabitur puer meus to sanabitur anima mea. [O'Connell ceremonial text](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/d-The%20Celebration%20of%20Mass-%20O%27Connel%201962_OCR.txt) explicitly distinguishes the celebrant's threefold private Domine non sum dignus and the distribution to the faithful. Do not mark a literal entire Gospel quotation where the rite adapts the noun.

### 4. An explicit 1962 rule: no second Confiteor within Mass at Communion

[Rubric 503](https://isidore.co/divinum/www/horas/Help/Rubrics/Missal1960%20rubrics.html): **When the faithful receive during Mass**, omit the communicants' Confiteor and corresponding absolution and proceed from the priest's own Communion to Ecce Agnus Dei + triple Domine non sum dignus. [Ritus X.6](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt) confirms the sequence. By contrast, [O'Connell outside-Mass Communion rite](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/d-The%20Celebration%20of%20Mass-%20O%27Connel%201962_OCR.txt) retains a Confiteor for Communion administered OUTSIDE Mass: these are materially different contexts. A modern optional second Confiteor setting must therefore, if retained by the separate product owner, be labelled a LOCAL OR OLDER CUSTOM, not a compulsory 1962 rubric. **Research-only handoff; no product UI switch changed.**

### 5. Seasonal dismissals and Last Gospel show why nineteenth-century commentary needs a 1962 check

[Guéranger's Ite missa est](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/) describes replacing the dismissal with Benedicamus Domino during penitential seasons. [1962 Rubricae 507](https://isidore.co/divinum/www/horas/Help/Rubrics/Missal1960%20rubrics.html) supersede that rule: ordinary Ite missa est is used even on penitential days, while Benedicamus is restricted to Masses followed by the specified procession/reposition, and Requiescant in pace to Requiem; 1962 Easter octave adds double Alleluia. [Rubricae 508](https://isidore.co/divinum/www/horas/Help/Rubrics/Missal1960%20rubrics.html) omit final blessing whenever Benedicamus or Requiescant has been said. [Rubricae 509–510](https://isidore.co/divinum/www/horas/Help/Rubrics/Missal1960%20rubrics.html) make Last Gospel conditional and enumerate its exceptions. In 1962, an unprocessed blanket assertion by Guéranger that an impeded Sunday's Gospel replaces John at the end is an **obsolete historical convention**, except where the adopted 1962 rubrics themselves explicitly provide a proper Palm Sunday Last Gospel.

### 6. Communion, prayer after Communion and the Last Gospel need different Bible classifications

[1 Cor 10:16–17](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/1_Corinthiens) articulates Communion in one Bread/Body, but it is not the **literal entire text** of the priest's self-Communion prayers. [Psalm 115 (Vulgate) / modern 116](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Psaumes) underlies the priest's Quid retribuam / calicem salutaris (existing MB25); ensure verse mapping is exact before publication. The [Communion antiphon and Postcommunion](https://isidore.co/divinum/www/horas/Help/Rubrics/Missal1960%20rubrics.html) are variable under the Mass Proper, with seasonal Alleluia and, on relevant Lenten ferias, an additional prayer over the people; don't invent one global Bible quote. [1962 Last Gospel, Rubr 509–510](https://isidore.co/divinum/www/horas/Help/Rubrics/Missal1960%20rubrics.html) regularly reads [John 1:1–14](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Jean) with a liturgical genuflection at Verbum caro factum est, but it is omitted in the explicit cases catalogued in 510. [Guéranger Dernier Evangile](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/) gives its later devotional development, not proof that this rite existed from apostolic times.

## Three usable initial French explanatory drafts — REVIEW ONLY

**L'Agnus Dei.** « Avant la Communion, l'Église reprend les paroles par lesquelles saint Jean-Baptiste désigne Jésus comme l'Agneau de Dieu (Jn 1,29), puis y ajoute ses demandes de miséricorde et de paix. Aux messes des défunts, ces demandes sont remplacées par une prière pour leur repos éternel. Il ne s'agit donc ni d'une citation identique de tout le chant dans l'Évangile ni d'un geste immuable à toutes les messes. » [Jean 1,29](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Jean); [Missel de 1962, Ritus X et XIII](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt).

**Le Domine non sum dignus.** « Le prêtre reprend, en les adaptant, les paroles du centurion qui demanda à Jésus la guérison de son serviteur (Mt 8,8). Dans la Messe, la demande s'applique à celui qui va recevoir le Seigneur : que mon âme soit guérie. L'humilité du centurion devient ainsi la prière de préparation à la sainte Communion. » [Matthieu 8,8](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Matthieu); [Missel, Ritus X](https://isidore.co/misc/Res%20pro%20Deo/ITOPL_OCR-layer-only/11a.%20Liturgy/Missale%20Romanum%201962_OCR.txt).

**Le dernier Évangile.** « Lorsqu'il est prescrit, le dernier Évangile fait entendre le prologue de saint Jean : le Verbe éternel est Dieu et s'est fait chair pour habiter parmi nous (Jn 1,1–14). Cette lecture conclut la Messe en rappelant le mystère de l'Incarnation. Le Missel de 1962 en prévoit toutefois l'omission à certaines célébrations, notamment après certaines processions, à la troisième Messe de Noël et à la Vigile pascale. » [Jean 1,1–14](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Jean); [Rubriques 509–510](https://isidore.co/divinum/www/horas/Help/Rubrics/Missal1960%20rubrics.html).
