Biblical correspondences / Baptism and rebirth

Naaman, water and grace

Two distinct kinds of relationship: the Gospel names an Old Testament person; a Father of the Church interprets his cleansing in the light of Baptism.

II / The Fathers

Saint Ambrose of Milan

De mysteriis, late fourth century. Original Latin excerpts from the Migne edition, explicitly separated from editorial explanations. No AI-composed speech or unverified English translation is presented as the saint's words.

§17

“non aquarum esse quod unusquisque mundatur, sed gratiae.”

Editorial label: the distinction between water and cleansing through grace.

§21

“tu autem baptizatus es in nomine Trinitatis”

Editorial label: Ambrose explicitly compares Naaman's washings with Christian baptism in the name of the Trinity.

Read the original Latin: De mysteriis, III.16–17 and IV.21

Source edition: Patrologia Latina 16 (1845), digital Latin transcription; original printed-page and modern critical-edition comparisons remain outstanding.

Historical liturgical context

These readings occur on Monday of the third week of Lent in the 1962 Proper. Dom Prosper Guéranger also discusses Naaman's sacramental significance in L'Année liturgique. His historical commentary is a separate witness, not a passage from Scripture or a direction for a 1962 ceremony.
