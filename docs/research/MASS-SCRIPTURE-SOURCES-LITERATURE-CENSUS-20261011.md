# The Mass through Scripture — source census and editorial method
**Initial verified bibliographic survey:** 11 October 2026  
**Status:** SOURCE_DISCOVERY_AND_SAMPLE_EXEGESIS_ONLY — NOT AN IMPLEMENTATION, NOT AN EXHAUSTIVE READ-THROUGH  
**Product scope:** the traditional Roman Mass as found in the 1962 Missal. Preserve distinct evidence labels; no additions to LIVE, Missal or production Bible Atlas in this phase.

## Thesis and safeguards

Explain prayers and actions of the traditional Roman Mass through **attested Scripture, patristic exegesis, the received Roman liturgical tradition, and critical liturgical history**. The aim is to **retrieve and annotate existing interpretations, not create new biblical parallels**. A late medieval allegory about a priest's gesture, an early witness to Eucharistic practice, and a quotation from Scripture are different kinds of evidence. Do not collapse them. The 1962 Missal must always be checked against pre-1962 commentary; additions and changes (including the St Joseph addition to the Canon and Holy Week) prohibit a blanket assumption of textual identity.

**Type of link:**
- `TEXT_QUOTATION` — exact or near-verbatim Scriptural wording in the current 1962 liturgical text; chapter/verse and edition required.
- `LITURGICAL_EXPLICIT_FIGURE` — the Roman liturgy itself invokes a named biblical person or sacrifice, with exact prayer phrase.
- `SCRIPTURAL_TYPOLOGY_PATRISTIC` — a named Father explicitly interprets a biblical episode as prefiguring Christ, Eucharist or Christian sacrifice; name chapter/section.
- `MAGISTERIAL_DOCTRINE` — a council/papal doctrinal text establishes a theological relation. This is not evidence that a particular Roman gesture originated in antiquity.
- `HISTORICAL_ATTESTATION` — a dated/located liturgical manuscript, text or historical study supports a development claim, with confidence stated.
- `MEDIEVAL_ALLEGORY` — interpretation proposed by a named medieval liturgical writer; must be signalled as an author's symbolic exposition, not an objectively attested ritual origin.
- `MODERN_SYNTHESIS` — an identified modern theological interpretation or contested reconstruction. Not silently upgraded into direct historical evidence.
- `DEVOTIONAL_ANALOGY` — spiritual reading; attributable to an author and not presented as liturgical institution or historical fact.

The editorial record for a link must contain: canonical liturgical prayer/phrase anchor (not unstable card number); exact Roman Latin quotation; Bible reference(s) and edition; exact original-text/printed-book location of the commentator; relation class; source URL; page or chapter; language; counterinterpretation if material; status `verified_primary / secondary_review / needs_full_text / rejected`. Build reversible **Mass ↔ Scripture** navigation only after this research review is complete.

## IV. Sample fully attributable chain: *Supra quae*

1. **Liturgical locus:** the 1962 Canon prayer *Supra quae propitio ac sereno vultu* explicitly names Abel, Abraham and Melchizedek. Start from the missal, preserving Latin phrases and location.
2. **Bible:** Genesis 4:4; 22:1–19; 14:18–20. Read each passage **as Scripture**, not as already containing all later Catholic sacrificial theology.
3. **Patristic interpretation:** Cyprian's letter to Caecilius §§3–4 explicitly interprets Melchizedek's bread-and-wine offering as a type of Christ. Ambrose *De mysteriis* ch.8 explicitly identifies Melchizedek and manna as prefigurations of Christian mysteries.
4. **Church doctrine:** Trent Session XXII chapters I–II places the Eucharist within Melchizedek's priestly typology and distinguishes the historical bloody offering of the Cross from its unbloody sacramental offering.
5. **Traditional Roman commentaries:** Guéranger supplies a coherent sacrificial comparison of Abel, Abraham and Melchizedek and passes to the heavenly altar of *Supplices te rogamus*. Gihr supplies more detailed formal theological treatment of the Mass prayers.
6. **Critical controls:** Fortescue/Jungmann must determine the textual and ceremonial development; do **not** assert Abel's original sacrifice or Melchizedek's historical location can be filmed accurately from later paintings or a regional map pin. A three-artwork transition is only an editorial illustration of sourced exegesis, subject to separate art-rights/crop review.

**Result:** an evidence-linked pathway may eventually join a Mass prayer to **three biblical narratives** and their attributed interpretations. Nothing in this initial bibliographic study authorises altering existing Roman Mass texts, prayers, rubrics, movement cues, or the source Bible Atlas.

The structure requires exact author/source URL and section locator per record; prospective 1962 text and biblical edition certification is marked incomplete on every record.

The new research identifies three especially useful source conflicts for future formation:
1. **Water and wine:** Cyprian's link between wine and Christ's Blood and between water and the faithful versus Guéranger's human/divine nature comparison. Both are attributed; neither silently replaces the other. [Cyprian letter to Caecilius](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050662.htm); [Guéranger Offertoire](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/).
2. **Supplices te rogamus:** Guéranger's literary image of the heavenly altar versus Aquinas's explicit denial that the consecrated species physically ascend to heaven. The artistic metaphor must never contradict theological explanation. [Aquinas ST III q.83 a.4 reply to objection 9](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4083.htm).
3. **The meaning of a gesture:** Durandus provides several alternative interpretations of the same three signs of the Cross and different ways to divide the Canon. These are evidence for *medieval exegetical diversity*, not a single ritual origin; Guéranger and Le Brun already warn against theatrical reconstruction. [Durandus IV chapter 36](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Rational_(Durand_de_Mende)/Volume_2/Quatri%C3%A8me_livre/Chapitre_36), [Le Brun's preface](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Lebrun_-_Explication_litt%C3%A9rale_historique_et_dogmatique_des_pri%C3%A8res_et_des_c%C3%A9r%C3%A9monies_de_la_messe_-_Tome_1_(1843).djvu/30).

The direct text of Gihr has been located [in the 1902 Internet Archive OCR](https://archive.org/download/holysacrificeofm00gihriala/holysacrificeofm00gihriala_djvu.txt), and his edition's preface is inspectable. **However a stable full chapter-by-chapter text extraction and independent claim collation were not completed in this pass.** Likewise Le Brun's complete work and Jungmann are not yet claim-by-claim attested.
