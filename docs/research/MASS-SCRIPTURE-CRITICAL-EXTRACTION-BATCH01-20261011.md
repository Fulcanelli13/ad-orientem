# Ad Orientem — Mass through Scripture: critical extraction, batch 1

**11 October 2026 | Research only — not implemented in the app**

## Scope and actual work

This is a **source-attested commentary extraction**, not an algorithmic search for Bible verses resembling a Roman prayer. The machine-readable [54-record evidence register](../../data/geography/research/mass-scripture-source-extractions-20261011.v1.json) spans 28 research phases of the 1962 Roman Mass and adjacent Eucharistic typology. Its work-level witnesses are **Guéranger (French, 1885), Aquinas (Latin author, New Advent English translation), Cyprian (English translation, letter to Caecilius), Ambrose (*De mysteriis*, English translation), Le Brun (French facsimile, 1843), and Durandus (French translation of medieval Latin, 1854)**. The 54 distinct records contain a concrete Latin phrase or rite, an attributable author's explanation, biblical references where meaningful, author/title URL, and the exact section or paragraph locator.

All 54 are explicitly **NOT CLEARED FOR PUBLICATION**. The authorial locations have been read selectively; there has **not** yet been a complete original-edition comparison against every phrase of the 1962 Missal, nor a complete collation with original Latin texts of the Fathers, nor a critical historical verification of medieval or nineteenth-century claims. An English translation of Cyprian or Ambrose is still a translation, not a verified original-Latin quotation. An existence record or table of contents for Gihr and Jungmann is **not** direct inspection of their arguments.

### What is already demonstrably sourced

**A. The foot of the altar and early prayers.** Guéranger's *Psaume Judica* explicitly accounts for choosing Psalm 42 in the Vulgate because of *Introibo ad altare Dei*. He relates the breast-striking at *mea culpa* to the publican in Luke 18. Ambrose, *De mysteriis* VIII §43 independently applies the same altar psalm to an initiated Christian approaching the Eucharistic table. **This is a theological and lexical convergence across authors, not evidence that Ambrose used the full 1962 prayers at the foot of the altar.** [Guéranger original French](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/), headings *PSAUME JUDICA / CONFITEOR*; [Ambrose §43](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3405.htm).

**B. The Offertory.** Guéranger's *OFFERTOIRE* explains *Suscipe sancte Pater* through the language of unblemished Old Testament victims, and *Deus qui humanae substantiae* through creation, Incarnation and 2 Peter 1:4; he explicitly reflects on the water and wine. Cyprian, writing to Caecilius (§§1–4, 11–13 in the linked edition), defends the mixed cup and uses a partly different symbolism: wine in relation to Christ's Blood, water as the gathered Christian people. **Both traditions must remain named, not flattened into one official meaning.** [Guéranger, Offertoire](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/); [Cyprian's source letter](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050662.htm). Under *LAVABO*, Guéranger separately identifies Psalm 25 (Vulgate) and admits the practical circumstance of washing after handling incense, alongside the moral sign of purification. Under *SUSCIPE SANCTA TRINITAS*, he reads the Offertory's explicit Passion–Resurrection–Ascension language as one economy of salvation. **These are distinct relations, not identical map events.**

**C. The Sanctus.** Guéranger identifies the *Sanctus* with Isaiah 6 and Revelation 4:8, while explaining the *Benedictus* through the acclamation of Christ's entry into Jerusalem. Aquinas, ST III q.83 a.4, independently distinguishes heavenly angelic praise from the acclamation of the children. The two visions of heaven must be **separate** Bible Atlas episodes; the entry into Jerusalem must be a **third** episode. [Guéranger, *SANCTUS*](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/); [Aquinas q.83 a.4](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4083.htm).

**D. The Roman Canon — explicit figures and three levels of interpretation.** In *Supra quae*, the priest actually names Abel, Abraham and Melchizedek. Guéranger supplies a theological comparison; Cyprian's Caecilius letter §4 already interprets the bread and wine of Melchizedek as a prefiguration of Christ's sacrifice; Ambrose VIII §§45–46 likewise draws the Melchizedek–Christ connection. **These have different authorities:** named liturgical words, original Genesis texts, patristic typology, and a later commentator's explanation. [Guéranger, *SUPRA QUAE*](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/); [Cyprian §4](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050662.htm); [Ambrose §§45–46](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3405.htm). Our Atlas has to preserve these relations distinctly.

**E. The heavenly altar is not a GPS location or a physical transfer cinematic.** Guéranger illustrates *Supplices te rogamus* with St John's heavenly vision. More importantly, Aquinas ST III q.83 a.4 reply to objection 9 explicitly denies that the priest prays for Christ's body or the sacramental species to be physically borne into heaven. He explains the angel, heavenly altar and presentation of prayers through theological senses. **This directly rules out depicting an angel physically carrying the consecrated Eucharist away from the altar as if it were the literal mechanism of the Roman Canon.** [Guéranger, *SUPPLICES TE ROGAMUS*](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/); [Aquinas, reply to objection 9](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4083.htm).

**F. Communion.** *Pater noster* corresponds to Matthew 6 and Luke 11; *Agnus Dei* uses the Baptist's words in John 1; *Domine non sum dignus* directly adapts the centurion's words in Matthew 8; the Communion preparation *Pacem relinquo vobis* is Christ's wording in John 14. Guéranger explains all four at the corresponding sections of the same French text; Aquinas independently discusses their liturgical purpose. **Only the first direct Scripture anchors should be promoted as literal Gospel-source links after edition collation**; broader Eucharistic theology must be separately attributed. [Guéranger, *PATER NOSTER / AGNUS DEI / COMMUNION*](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/); [Aquinas ST III q.83 a.4](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/4083.htm).

**G. Emmaus and the fraction: recovered medieval explanation, not invented.** Durandus, *Rationale divinorum officiorum* IV chapter 51, relates recognition at the breaking of bread in Luke 24 to the fraction of the Host. This is an authentic **medieval authorial reading**, but it does **not** establish that Christ instituted the precise Roman fraction ceremony at Emmaus. The same chapter gives several historical ritual sequences and symbolic interpretations for commixture. [Durandus, original French translation, chapter 51](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Rational_(Durand_de_Mende)/Volume_2/Quatri%C3%A8me_livre/Chapitre_51), especially § around the breaking of bread and §§XVI–XIX.

## Key scholarly corrections

**1. Guéranger explicitly denies total theatrical re-enactment.** In his opening exposition, he says not all Mass actions can be equated with distinct incidents of the Passion. This directly opposes designing a forced cinematic parallel for every gesture. [Guéranger introduction](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/), paragraphs before *PSAUME JUDICA*.

**2. Pierre Le Brun provides the complementary methodological critique.** In the preface to his work, he criticises attempts to reduce the meaning of gestures to acting out the words and also critiques a purely physicalist account of their origin. Religious gestures can express interior disposition without being literal dramatic reconstruction. [1843 facsimile, preface p. xv onward](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Lebrun_-_Explication_litt%C3%A9rale_historique_et_dogmatique_des_pri%C3%A8res_et_des_c%C3%A9r%C3%A9monies_de_la_messe_-_Tome_1_(1843).djvu/30).

**3. Durandus is valuable precisely because he preserves multiple alternatives.** In Book IV chapter 36, he enumerates ways of dividing the Canon into 11, 12, six or five units and then gives several **different** explanations for the priest's three signs of the Cross, including Trinitarian meaning, Passion associations and the periods Adam–Noah–Abraham–Moses. This tells us not to use the number of Canon subdivisions or a proposed medieval symbolism as evidence that a modern 48-card Mass layout is historically normative. [Durandus, chapter 36 §§I–VIII](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Rational_(Durand_de_Mende)/Volume_2/Quatri%C3%A8me_livre/Chapitre_36).

**4. The words *mysterium fidei* require an edition-aware distinction.** Durandus Book IV chapter 42 §XX cites Innocent III's observation that these exact words do not appear in the Gospel institution narratives. The received Roman formula and the scriptural four-witness account must be linked through an explicit **liturgical adaptation**, not mislabeled a verbatim Bible quotation. [Durandus, chapter 42 §XX](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Rational_(Durand_de_Mende)/Volume_2/Quatri%C3%A8me_livre/Chapitre_42).

**5. Do not trust unverified historical attributions.** Guéranger repeats sweeping claims that the Canon remained unchanged everywhere and attributes particular words to Alexander I and Leo and Gregory. Some such claims are too broad or need documentary scrutiny. The registry treats these as **historical holds**, not canonical explanations. The independent critical work of Fortescue, Jungmann and early Roman sacramentaries must decide the origin questions.

**6. The source authors themselves explain their limitations.** The introduction of Guéranger's 1885 edition candidly states that these were notes taken during conferences, sometimes imperfectly transcribed and without scholarly apparatus. Gihr, in the 1902 English preface to *The Holy Sacrifice of the Mass*, says his aim is dogmatic and ascetical rather than comprehensive historical research, and wishes to avoid artificial subjective explanations. These are reasons for **using** the books with care, not discarding them. [Guéranger editorial preface](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/); [Gihr 1902 original OCR](https://archive.org/download/holysacrificeofm00gihriala/holysacrificeofm00gihriala_djvu.txt) (first pages directly located; chapter-text OCR retrieval inconsistent).

## Proposed structure of the eventual evidence network

Each prayer can reference multiple **distinct, reversible** nodes:
1. `1962_ROMAN_MASS_PHRASE` — exact Latin text/rubric, original source anchor.
2. `BIBLE_PASSAGE` — exact canon + original-language variant, Gospel/Genesis etc.
3. `AUTHORIAL_INTERPRETATION` — named Church Father, schoolman, medieval author or historian and **direct source context**.
4. `LITURGICAL_HISTORY_EVENT` — only when independent liturgical documentary evidence establishes the historical date/rite.
5. `SPATIAL_OR_GENEALOGICAL_REFERENCE` — one of the existing validated Atlas people, places and sequences; *not* an automatically created pin.
6. `ARTWORK_REFERENCE` — illustration of a named interpretation, never itself historical proof.

No automatic "one prayer → one Scripture scene" implication; many prayers require zero scenes, some one, and *Supra quae* at least three. No historical dates for Creation/Fall as if documentary chronology; no exact coordinates for the heavenly altar, Moriah from unverified traditions or a reconstructed Last Supper scene.
