## III. Strict masterpiece-first visual policy

**Acquisition phase 1: approximately 15–25 outstanding anchor works**, not 70 images or 44 artworks just because the date list contains 44 possible subject themes. Prioritize exceptional Christian art for Nativity, Epiphany, Annunciation, Presentation, Lenten meditation, Palm Sunday, Last Supper, Crucifixion, Resurrection, Ascension, Pentecost, Trinity, Corpus Christi, Sacred Heart, Saint Joseph, Assumption, Immaculate Conception, Christ the King, All Saints/All Souls, and Advent; reuse only when iconographically honest.

A source can be famous and still unsuitable for a small phone hero; an important icon or authenticated devotional image is not necessarily a museum oil painting. Iconographic accuracy, excellent composition, emotional/devotional force, and recognizable artistic quality are the hard artistic filter. We should actively research masterpieces by major Christian artists and major ecclesiastical collections—rather than assigning any technically acceptable museum picture to each name/date. **User should see 1–3 exceptional options for each important theme, not broad catalogues or mediocre fallback rows.**

**Holding rule:** approved painting remains on Home until the next significant date **with an equally worthy approved painting**. It may remain one week, two weeks or longer. Never switch merely because the Calendar resolves another Class I Sunday or ordinary saint. If a Class I day has no exceptional artwork, the visible image stays, while the liturgical headline shows the day's **correct** celebration; its art caption always retains the **actual depicted subject**, avoiding false feast attribution. No "image unavailable" placeholder and no weekly random shuffling.

**Release:** user aesthetic selection first; then source original, reproduction jurisdiction, cropping and phone suitability. The earlier 44 editorial selections are **legacy research**, not a binding approval for this stricter homepage standard. We must not automatically publish them. The existing Rosary, Stations, Angelus, and other live devotion art remain separate and unchanged during this planning phase.

## IV. Implementation and future verification

1. Freeze the **70 dated occasions** (53 Class I + 17 optional highlights), their 1962 observed identities and optional art subject cues. Review additions/removals *before image searches*.
2. Group multiple dates into narrative/seasonal anchor art slots, reduce acquisition work to the strongest 15–25 first.
3. For each anchor theme, research true internationally significant old-master pieces or primary devotional images, with historical iconography and attractive phone framing. Do not acquire all candidates; present only shortlists.
4. Reintroduce those works to the homepage via an event-driven **hold-until-replaced** selector that leaves the Mass/Calendar DayResolver as canonical and always uses the correct caption. No daily random artworks, no class-III/IV gap-coverage work.
5. Add local Class I patronal/dedication observances only after the relevant territorial/institute liturgical calendar and precedence are documented; this 2026 universal listing does not purport to include them.

**Decision to confirm:** This is the occasion list, **not** an image-gallery request. No new artwork has been retrieved or approved under the reset.
