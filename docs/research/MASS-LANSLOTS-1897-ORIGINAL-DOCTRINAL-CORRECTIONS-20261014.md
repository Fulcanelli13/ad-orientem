## 1. Inspiration of Sacred Scripture is not identical to approval of liturgical composition

**EN.** In chapter XIX, Lanslots speaks as though phrases of the Institution narrative not found in the Evangelists had the same inspiration as canonical Scripture (OCR 6353–6356). The Canon is venerable and authoritative, but that alone does not give every later liturgical phrase the distinctive divine authorship and canonical inspiration of the biblical books. This distinction is not an innovation of Vatican II: Leo XIII's *Providentissimus Deus* (1893) and Pius XII's *Divino afflante Spiritu* (1943) already teach divine inspiration of the **canonical Scriptures**; *Dei Verbum* §11 (1965) likewise teaches it. [Lanslots, ch. XIX](https://archive.org/stream/illustratedexpla00lans/illustratedexpla00lans_djvu.txt) · [Leo XIII (1893)](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_18111893_providentissimus-deus.html) · [Pius XII (1943)](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_30091943_divino-afflante-spiritu.html) · [Vatican II (1965)](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html).

**FR — projet.** Les formules liturgiques reçues possèdent leur propre autorité, mais leur approbation n'en fait pas des textes bibliques canoniquement inspirés. Cette distinction est déjà affirmée, avant Vatican II, par Léon XIII et Pie XII; elle est reprise par *Dei Verbum*. [Lanslots, ch. XIX](https://archive.org/stream/illustratedexpla00lans/illustratedexpla00lans_djvu.txt) · [Pie XII, version française (1943)](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/fr/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_30091943_divino-afflante-spiritu.html) · [Dei Verbum §11](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html).

## 2. The angel in *Supplices te rogamus* — an attributed dispute

**EN.** At `AO.CANON.10` / `AO.SM.C0187`, Lanslots states that Guéranger understands the holy angel as Christ, the Father's emissary. He also reports an alternative created-angel reading and says any more precise identification would be conjectural. Thus the two actual arguments belong in the debate; neither the individual angel's identity nor a physical conveyance of the consecrated Species to heaven is established by the Latin prayer. Existing Guéranger owner: `FRG44`, to be **enriched**, not duplicated. [Lanslots, ch. XX, OCR 7065–7138](https://archive.org/stream/illustratedexpla00lans/illustratedexpla00lans_djvu.txt) · [Guéranger, original French, *Supplices*](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/).

**FR — projet.** Guéranger identifie l'Ange au Christ envoyé par le Père; Lanslots expose aussi l'interprétation selon laquelle il s'agit d'un ange créé. L'identification individuelle reste conjecturale. Ni l'une ni l'autre ne transforme la prière en description d'un déplacement matériel des saintes Espèces. [Lanslots, ch. XX](https://archive.org/stream/illustratedexpla00lans/illustratedexpla00lans_djvu.txt) · [Guéranger, `Supplices`](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/).

## 3. *Hæc commixtio* does not consecrate the Eucharist again

**EN.** At `AO.SM.C0221`, Lanslots cites Bellarmine's account of a renewed sacramental *signification* at the commingling of already consecrated Species. It does **not** assert a second transubstantiation or another moment when Christ first becomes present. Source-critical owner `FRG57` already handles this distinction in French. [Lanslots, ch. XXIII, OCR 8378–8418](https://archive.org/stream/illustratedexpla00lans/illustratedexpla00lans_djvu.txt) · [Guéranger, original French](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/).

**FR — projet.** Le mot `consecratio` dans la formule du mélange n'indique pas une nouvelle transsubstantiation : Bellarmin, tel que le rapporte Lanslots, y distingue un sens sacramentel renouvelé. Il convient de conserver l'explication antérieure déjà rattachée à la même prière. [Lanslots, ch. XXIII](https://archive.org/stream/illustratedexpla00lans/illustratedexpla00lans_djvu.txt) · [Guéranger](https://www.domgueranger.net/explication-des-prieres-de-la-sainte-messe-2/).

## 4. *Communicantes*: St Joseph — precise 1962 amendment

**EN.** Lanslots's chapter XVII reproduces the nineteenth-century `Communicantes` without St Joseph (OCR 5451–5480); Guéranger's earlier text is similar. **That is not a defect in the current reader.** The Sacred Congregation of Rites' *Novis hisce temporibus* of **13 November 1962**, effective **8 December 1962**, ordered the insertion of `sed et beati Ioseph eiusdem Virginis Sponsi`. The current source text correctly retains this in `AO.SM.C0159` / `AO.CANON.03`. An edition printed before the amendment and the liturgical practice governed by the amendment must be dated distinctly. [Lanslots, ch. XVII](https://archive.org/stream/illustratedexpla00lans/illustratedexpla00lans_djvu.txt) · [Sacred Congregation of Rites, French decree text preserved by ICRSP](https://www.icrsp.org/Pages/mois-saint-joseph.php?groupe=magistere&nom=decret) · [USCCB historical corroboration](https://www.usccb.org/prayer-and-worship/the-mass/order-of-mass/liturgy-of-the-eucharist/saint-joseph-in-the-eucharistic-prayers).

**FR — projet.** Lanslots ne nomme pas saint Joseph dans le `Communicantes` qu'il reproduit en 1897. Cependant, le décret `Novis hisce temporibus` du 13 novembre 1962 prescrivit les mots `sed et beati Ioseph eiusdem Virginis Sponsi`, à partir du **8 décembre 1962**. Le texte actuel de la prière doit donc conserver son nom. [Congrégation des Rites, décret français](https://www.icrsp.org/Pages/mois-saint-joseph.php?groupe=magistere&nom=decret) · [Lanslots](https://archive.org/stream/illustratedexpla00lans/illustratedexpla00lans_djvu.txt).

## 5. Typology, historical explanation and pastoral discipline

The laying-on of hands in Leviticus 8:14 supplies Lanslots's analogy for the extended hands at `Hanc igitur`; it does not by itself prove the Roman gesture originated from that verse. Melchisedech and Psalm 109:4 furnish traditional Eucharistic typology at `Supra quæ`; Genesis 14:18 must not be expanded into detailed unrecorded ancient ceremonial. Lanslots's apostolic-Latin chronology and ascription of Canon wording to Pope Alexander I likewise need independent historical witnesses. [Lanslots, chs. VI, XIII, XVIII–XX, OCR 1619–1658, 3684–3706, 6120–6169, 6353–6372, 7022–7059](https://archive.org/stream/illustratedexpla00lans/illustratedexpla00lans_djvu.txt).

Similarly, his discussion of naming the dead in historical diptychs, funeral discipline and private supplication is not a ready-made 1962 or contemporary rule. The theological exposition of `Memento etiam` should preserve intercession, mercy and charity without collapsing public liturgical acts into private prayer or adjudicating a particular deceased person's salvation. [Lanslots, ch. XXI, OCR 7255–7314](https://archive.org/stream/illustratedexpla00lans/illustratedexpla00lans_djvu.txt).
