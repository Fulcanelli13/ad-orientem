## Evidence and boundaries

This is a continuation of [pass 6](PRAYER-NOVENAS-RECITATION-PASS6-20261010.md) and [the source inventory v2](../../data/pray/novena-source-review.v2.json). The new [v3 source-review register](../../data/pray/novena-source-review.v3.json) consolidates **all 16 novenas**; it is editorial evidence, not a second runtime corpus or an additional reader.

**Terminology:** A *digital English anchor check* compares the existing prayer text's opening, closing, title/day identity and section order against the cited online transcription. It is not a complete word-by-word collation of printed pages; no original printed facsimiles, parallel original French editions or official ecclesiastical approval updates are inferred from the internet transcription.

## Added 36 of 72 historical day-prayer checks

### Moran, 1883: Immaculate Conception (9)

The [1883 source section](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Catholic_Prayer_Book_and_Manual_of_Meditations/Novena_in_Preparation_for_the_Feast_of_the_Immaculate_Conception) explicitly enumerates proper daily prayers for **29 November to 7 December**. The app's nine English openings and endings follow the online book transcription and its day order. Source lines are identified individually in the v3 review. The existing sequence fix from pass 6 — common opening, **nine Aves and one Gloria**, day prayer, Litany of Loreto — was retained.

**Still incomplete:** Moran's source supplies the alternate `Tota pulchra` text followed by historical collects; the production reader uses its canonical Loreto Litany and links to the 1883 original rather than silently modernizing a collect naming Pope Leo. The original French edition of these nine prayers has not been located or certified.

### Hammer, 1909: Annunciation, Seven Sorrows, Assumption (27)

[Hammer's *Mary, Help of Christians* (Benziger, 1909; Gutenberg English text)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33671/33671-h/33671-h.htm) prints nine distinct **Prayer** bodies in Section III (Annunciation), nine in Section IV (Seven Sorrows), and nine in Section V (Assumption). All 27 app English day-prayer openings/endings and the section assignments were checked against the Gutenberg transcription. They materially agree at these identified boundaries, but that does **not** make the entire line-by-line prayer or translation independently certified.

Each of these 27 source days *also* includes its own historical **MEDITATION** and **PRACTICE**, not merely a one-sentence theme. Those **27 pairs remain absent from the in-app prayer body**. The user's short bilingual day guide remains editorial, not falsely attributed to Hammer. Each day now has a small collapsed **Original meditation & practice / Méditation et pratique historiques** disclosure directly beside its proper prayer in both Simple and Guided readings. This presents the exact 1909 English section title and a link to the original source containing the longer meditation and resolution. The link reaches the historical book, not a guaranteed direct text fragment; it requires finding the displayed day heading. The book remains externally hosted, not offline-packaged.

Hammer's Assumption meditations reflect devotional narrative; references to Mary's death in the old source must not be presented as independent dogmatic definitions.

### Moran, 1883: two repeated-form prayers

The [Corpus Christi form](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Catholic_Prayer_Book_and_Manual_of_Meditations/Novena_for_the_Festival_of_Corpus_Christi) begins Wednesday in Pentecost week, while the [Sacred Heart form](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Catholic_Prayer_Book_and_Manual_of_Meditations/Novena_for_the_Feast_of_the_Sacred_Heart) begins on Corpus Christi. These are **one repeated original prayer each**, not eighteen original day prayers. The app's two English repeat bodies correspond in opening and ending to those online sections. The Sacred Heart Wikisource text includes suspicious transcription words (e.g. “next for those pure” rather than likely “not for those pure”); the app's smoother English is NOT certified as a facsimile correction. Further print collation is required.
