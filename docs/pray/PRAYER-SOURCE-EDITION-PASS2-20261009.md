## Confirmed issues and actions

1. **Historical source drawers were incomplete.** The Stations of the Cross, Seven Penitential Psalms, Seven Words and Forty Hours displayed bibliographic names without direct clickable original-document text. The Prayer-owned source helper now supplies specific HTTPS historical witnesses while explicitly distinguishing their purposes (historical St Alphonsus English method; separately selected Bible edition; English meditation witness; historical Forty Hours account).
2. **Seven Words ends at the seventh Word, not at the end of the webpage.** The [1889 Baltimore Manual](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Manual_of_Prayers_for_the_Use_of_the_Catholic_Laity/The_Devotion_of_the_Seven_Words_upon_the_Cross) contains a Marian conclusion after the seventh meditation. The old parser attached *all subsequent text* to the seventh Word. The new reader separates it into an expandable **Historical concluding prayers (English)** drawer instead of misrepresenting it as Seventh Word meditation. Parsing fails visibly if the seven headings or appendix boundary cannot be recognized; a cached old remote witness uses a new key to prevent stale unbounded text.
3. **French Act of Hope is an apparent source anomaly, not an accidental app substitution.** The [Holy See’s 2005 French Compendium, Appendix A](https://www.vatican.va/archive/compendium_ccc/documents/archive_2005_compendium-ccc_fr.html) itself ends the *Acte d'espérance* with “Dans cette foi, puis-je vivre et mourir.” [English Compendium](https://www.vatican.va/archive/compendium_ccc/documents/archive_2005_compendium-ccc_en.html) ends “In this hope”; Latin says “In hac spe”. This batch preserves the attested French text **verbatim** and supplies a concise bilingual discrepancy note inside the prayer’s source disclosure. Replacing `foi` with `espérance` without a separately attested corrected edition would breach the rule against silent modernization.
4. **Forty Hours bibliographic overclaim removed.** The Prayer footnote formerly treated an unlinked *Saint Andrew Daily Missal* (1951) as authority for special Forty Hours ceremonial. The verified linked [1889 Baltimore Manual devotion chapter](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Manual_of_Prayers_for_the_Use_of_the_Catholic_Laity/The_Devotion_of_the_Forty_Hours%27_Adoration) documents a historical devotion and prayer aids; it does **not** establish that every church uses one universal public rite, or that the historical indulgence grants remain current.
5. **Seven Penitential Psalms edition distinction.** Historical prayer books support the sequence: [Catholic's pocket prayer-book](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Catholic%27s_pocket_prayer-book/The_Seven_Penitential_Psalms), and the [1889 Baltimore Manual contents](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Manual_of_Prayers_for_the_Use_of_the_Catholic_Laity). The displayed biblical verses remain the responsibility of the **central Scripture service** and chosen Catholic Bible edition; the historical sequence witness must not be mislabelled as the provenance of that Bible translation.
6. **Traditional Stations text.** The [1889 Baltimore Manual](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Manual_of_Prayers_for_the_Use_of_the_Catholic_Laity/The_Stations_of_the_Cross) specifically prints the St Alphonsus method. It supplies an English historical witness, **not** a completed word-for-word check of the French reader text.
