# Fortescue printed p.257 — targeted collation of the 1913 scan (2026-10-15)

**Scope:** original article *Canon of the Mass* (first published 1908), **1913 Knights of Columbus special-edition** printed witness `ED-CE1913-V3-KOC-SPECIAL`. Target print p.257 / DjVu 305 and top of print p.258 / DjVu 306. This research document does **not** certify the entire historical page.

Primary images: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Catholic_Encyclopedia,_volume_3.djvu/305 and https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Catholic_Encyclopedia,_volume_3.djvu/306. The page-based Wikisource text expressly says **not proofread**. The [New Advent transcription](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/03255c.htm) derives from the 1908 work and is a separate transcription; it must not overwrite the 1913 printed witness.

## Concrete findings

1. **First Epistle of Clement reference:** on printed p.257, the section about priestly ranks is referenced **(xl)**, i.e. chapter 40. New Advent has **(xi)**, i.e. chapter 11. The independent text of **1 Clement 40** at https://ccel.org/ccel/clement_rome/first_epistle_to_the_corinthians/anf01.ii.ii.xl.html contains the high-priest / priests / Levites sentence and corroborates **XL**. This is a substantive source-address defect in a widely accessed secondary transcription. The current p257 draft already correctly uses `(xl)` and must continue to do so. Do not global-replace XI→XL in other works.
2. **English edition wording:** the examined 1913 witness includes **“before this service”** in the early Eucharistic narrative; New Advent has **“before the service”**. Keep the 1913 wording in the 1913 edition, without inferring which text the original 1908 printing contains.
3. **OCR error versus original:** the raw Wikisource layer shows **“fifth eentury”**, **“frill be Been”**, **“at any ■ it side Palestine”**, **“Pope Victot”**, and incorrect reproduction of the Probst and patrologia references. These are *OCR* errors, not scholarly emendations to Fortescue. Proposed repairs remain preliminary until independent image proofreading.
4. **Pseudo-Ambrose quotation:** New Advent renders *coporis* for a reading corresponding to *corporis* in the preliminary 1913 print check; this is a likely transcription typo. The *hanc* versus *hunc panem* case now provisionally favours **hanc** on the image, as separately recorded below; *coelum* versus *cælum*, ligature use, source references, italics and punctuation have not been certified character by character. Preserve the full Latin quote as **held**, not clean-room publishable text; philological grammar alone must not manufacture the 1913 letterforms.
5. **Justin, First Apology locator:** unproofread page OCR is corrupt, but the 1913 printed image appears to show `(lxv–lxvi)`, agreeing with New Advent's `(lxv-lxvi)`. The earlier provisional `(lxv–lxvii)` was unsupported and has been corrected to **LXV–LXVI**. This is a targeted provisional collation; final high-resolution verification of the original glyphs and parentheses is still required.
6. **Patristic witness versus historical quotation:** Fortescue's printed quotation begins *Fac nos hanc oblationem*, while editions of the quoted `De sacramentis` IV read *Fac nobis hanc oblationem*. Independent evidence: [Dictionnaire de théologie catholique](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionnaire_de_th%C3%A9ologie_catholique/MESSE_VIII._La_messe_dans_la_liturgie_7._Le_De_sacramentis_du_Pseudo_Ambroise) and [modern textual study, pp.39ff](https://publicacions.iec.cat/repository/pdf/00000174/00000057.pdf). This is **not** licence to silently rewrite Fortescue. Two textual witnesses with different provenance must remain distinct and any detailed textual variant claims must remain tied to the named edition.
7. **Physical page continuity:** the bottom right of printed p.257 ends in a sentence after *“He also says that at”*; printed p.258 starts by continuing that sentence. The page turn is not a paragraph break; no invented punctuation, citations or text may be inserted into the join. The source chapter continues well beyond p.258.

## Further direct-image checks — 15 October 2026

- **1913 printed historical quotation: `hanc panem sanctum` (provisional image reading).** The quarantined working draft previously read `hunc panem sanctum`, likely a grammatical normalization. The original page image appears to have **`hanc`**, corroborated by New Advent's separate text. The provisional draft now preserves **`hanc`**. It is not legitimate to amend the 1913 print to `hunc` merely because the latter is grammatically more natural. Final independent character-by-character proofreading is still required.
- **Other author/edition lead:** a digitized text of Fortescue's *The Mass: A Study of the Roman Liturgy*, originally published as a **different work**, has `hunc panem sanctum` at a corresponding discussion (its §4/p.67 in the digitized version). Its exact *original print page has not been inspected*; the record therefore treats it solely as a **distinct-work research lead**, not as a second edition that corrects the 1913 encyclopedia.
- **Intra-article quotation discrepancy in the original 1913 scan:** printed p.257 / DjVu image 305 has `Fac nos hanc oblationem` in the historical *De sacramentis* quotation, while **printed p.259 / DjVu image 307**, in the same Fortescue article, has `Fac nobis hanc oblationem` when Fortescue returns to the `Quam oblationem` argument. Both short forms have now been directly located in the original scanned-page images: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Catholic_Encyclopedia,_volume_3.djvu/305 and https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Catholic_Encyclopedia,_volume_3.djvu/307. The p.259 passage is **not fully transcribed or human-certified**. Do not treat the two independent occurrences as one source locator, erase the difference, or infer a preferred patristic text from Fortescue's variation alone.
- **Critical patristic edition:** a published study cites O. Faller (ed.), *De sacramentis* IV.5.21, *CSEL* 73 (Vienna, 1955), p.55, with `Fac nobis … scriptam … quod est figura`. Fortescue's 1913 printed quotation uses `Fac nos … adscriptam, ratam … quod figura est`. This is a substantive difference in *quoted textual form*, not a proof that the 1913 text is wrong. The source study is available at https://www.lascuolacattolica.it/ojs/index.php/lsc/article/download/3891/2231 ; **the 1955 original printed edition's page image and apparatus have not yet been directly collated**.
- **Print paragraph boundary, not software block boundary:** printed p.257 runs `left in Greek. The Creed was …` continuously within the discussion of Latin. The working text had split this into P257-06 and P257-07 for convenient editing; those two IDs are now explicitly linked to the same preliminary print-paragraph group. P257-08/09 and P257-09/10 may also have uninterrupted print text, but their exact paragraph boundaries remain uncertain and **are not certified**.
- **Physical page turn:** p.257/image 305 ends in mid-sentence; p.258/image 306 resumes that sentence. The physical continuity has been located, but the complete text is still not proofread or approved for merge.

These checks constitute **targeted corrections and structural progress**, not a claim of a fully verified Latin quotation or completed first page.
