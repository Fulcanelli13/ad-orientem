# Catholic customs — Italian regional feast and votive bread research, non-duplicate wave 02 (23 October 2026)

This is a **regional Italian documentary research intake**, entirely separate from Ad Orientem's existing Calendar/runtime and the parallel household-author intake. Before writing, draft PR #1572 already indexed 110 Italian UNESCO shoulder-machine details for **Nola, Sassari, Palmi, Viterbo**, plus 96 Sicilian Holy Week details for **Enna, Trapani, Caltanissetta**. **None of those festivals was re-extracted.** The additional source material covers Cocullo (Abruzzo), Gubbio (Umbria), Salemi and other Sicilian towns, and is retained with individual locality and source lineage.

## Evidence — 9 online institutional/community witnesses

### Cocullo — San Domenico Abate and the serpent procession

Direct institutional witnesses:
- [ICPI, San Domenico a Cocullo](https://icpi.cultura.gov.it/san-domenico-a-cocullo/) — 2021-era ethnographic synthesis, HTML lines **36–53**, including the pre-/post-2012 festival-date shift, live serpents, saint's statue, votive breads, pilgrim departures and locally reported magical protections.
- [ICCD national catalogue, Cocullo record 13iccd_modi_0346276630641](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/DemoEthnoAnthropologicalHeritage/13iccd_modi_0346276630641), HTML lines **16–25**, distinguishing the post-1984 church setting and relic devotion.

**21 source-detail rows**. These are components of **one wider patronal event**, not 21 distinct Catholic customs. Historic killing of snakes is **discontinued**; local protective or curative efficacy claims (stones, snake omens, bell cord pulled by teeth) are historical folk beliefs, **not endorsed Catholic sacramental instructions**. The source explicitly distinguishes the *first Thursday of May* from the more recent **1 May** date. No 1962 Roman liturgical approval is inferred.

### Gubbio — St Ubaldo's Ceri, guild and confraternity succession

Direct inspected sources:
- [ICPI, Sant'Ubaldo a Gubbio](https://icpi.cultura.gov.it/santubaldo-a-gubbio/), lines **48–51**, showing raised wooden Ceri, assigned saint statues, procession order and route.
- [Umbria Tourism, Festa dei Ceri](https://www.umbriatourism.it/it/w/festa-dei-ceri-a-gubbio), lines **111–120**.
- [SIUSA: Confraternita dei muratori di Gubbio](https://siusa-archivi.cultura.gov.it/cgi-bin/siusa/pagina.pl?Chiave=47489&TipoPag=prodente), lines **61–63**: nineteenth-century confraternity statutory revival.
- [SIUSA: Università dei muratori ... Gubbio](https://siusa-archivi.cultura.gov.it/cgi-bin/pagina.pl?Chiave=47325&RicProgetto=reg-umb&TipoPag=prodente), lines **57–65**: 1891 organisation and captain selection; Fascist-era interruption and 1946 resumption.

**21 source-detail rows** relating to **one Ceri/St Ubaldo regional celebration** and its historically distinct institutional phases, not 21 separate customs. Archival synopsis records **1851, 1891 and 1946** as reported dates; original 1851 confraternity statutes and 1891 civic contract were **not independently inspected**. The three sculpted patron saint objects should not each automatically become a new festival identity; organisation, guild symbolism, processional and devotional components remain distinct subrecords.

### Salemi and Sicilian bread-charity feast customs

Source witnesses:
- [Museo del Pane Rituale Salemi — Salemi breads](https://museodelpaneritualesalemi.jimdofree.com/pani-salemitani-bread-of-salemi/) — directly read Italian narrative, actions line-located **31–203**; the parallel English translation on the same webpage is **not another source**.
- [Museo del Pane Rituale Salemi — Sicily regional collection](https://museodelpaneritualesalemi.jimdofree.com/pani-siciliani/) — regional detail descriptions line-located **39–264**; one museum compiler may reuse local material, and each town is not independently verified as a distinct author/eyewitness.
- [Pro Loco Salemi, Città dei pani](https://www.prolocosalemi.it/men%C3%B9/scopri-il-borgo/citt%C3%A0-dei-pani), lines **20–22**, a community witness retelling some same Feast of St Joseph acts, not certified independent corroboration.

**72 source-detail rows**: Salemi St Anthony Abbot Jan 17 animal bread/feed blessings; St Blaise Feb 3 throat blessing and Cuddureddi/Cavadduzzi; St Joseph March 19 home votive altars and meals with three Holy Family child participants; St Francis of Paola thirteen youth participants; St Anthony of Padua feast bread; St Nicholas of Tolentino September marked breads (museum reports a 1779 Augustinian account); All Souls breads given to the poor; St Elizabeth Hungary flower breads; St Nicholas Bari product-bread blessing; Easter egg breads; and locality-specific material and dinner variants in **Chiusa Sclafani, Santa Margherita Belice, Camporeale, Alcamo, Castelvetrano, Valguarnera Caropepe, Santa Croce Camerina, Poggioreale, Prizzi, Mezzojuso and Palazzo Adriano**.

This is not merely “St Joseph tables” repeated seventy-two times. Multiple patronal feasts, almsgiving, agricultural thanksgivings and locally named bread forms are separately recorded, but **their distinctness as canonical customs is not adjudicated**. The Salemi museum describes certain protective bread beliefs and pre-1968 practices; they remain **history/folklore only**, not recommended uses. Museum references to **1542 St Blaise**, **1779 St Nicholas Tolentino**, and **1832 Santa Croce Camerina** have not been verified in their cited original archival witnesses. Modern statements must not be backdated to the 1962 Missal or Roman Ritual. The St Elizabeth November date and any conflicting feast-day versions require explicit calendar reconciliation later.
