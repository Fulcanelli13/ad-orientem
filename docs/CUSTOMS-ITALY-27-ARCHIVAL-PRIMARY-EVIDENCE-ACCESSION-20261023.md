## Strongest verified archival discoveries

### Gubbio: original Ceri guild and confraternity material

The Italian State archival system's [SIUSA inventory of the Università dei Muratori](https://siusa-archivi.cultura.gov.it/cgi-bin/siusa/pagina.pl?Chiave=310919&RicProgetto=reg-umb&TipoPag=comparc) identifies **44 buste** (boxes), broadly dated **8 February 1761–2006**, with an institutional gap stated between 1797 and 1851. The key original-source accession is specifically the **1761–1797 “Libro dei consigli della magnifica Arte dei muratori della città di Gubbio,” busta 20**. That is an actual shelfmark cited by the archival authority, not a guessed record. Related holdings include:

- Statute series **1851–2005, one busta**, exact box identifier not given in public summary; retrieve episcopal approval conditions and compare distinct versions.
- Captain and **bussolo** (ballot vessel) records **1881–2005, one busta**; investigate changes in captain choice.
- Council acts **1761–2001, five buste**; identify dated guild agreements, Mass celebrations and festival changes.
- Financial records **1840–2004, seven buste**; evaluate gifts, Ceri repair, devotional objects and feast expenses while separating civic costs from prayer.
- Historical Ceri documents **1798–2005, one busta**; track status through suppressed guild to reconstitution.
- Mass-related administrative papers **1865–2003, one busta**; exact clergy/ritual ownership unresolved.
- Additional sources include two photo buste, two modern Ceri-record buste, six `tavola bona` buste and **11 feast-dinner notebooks outside the stated 44-box inventory**.

[The 1851 Confraternity SIUSA profile](https://siusa-archivi.cultura.gov.it/cgi-bin/siusa/pagina.pl?Chiave=47489&TipoPag=prodente) says Bishop Giuseppe Pecci officially recognized the guild successor confraternity, which adopted statutes and resumed Mass, public election of captains and the confraternal meal. [The later Università SIUSA profile](https://siusa-archivi.cultura.gov.it/cgi-bin/pagina.pl?Chiave=47325&RicProgetto=reg-umb&TipoPag=prodente) cites the printed **1887 statutes**, 1891 city-organizing arrangement and guild reconstitution in **1946**. Each claim requires its exact dated original text before it can become a verified history assertion in an Ad Orientem custom.

A modern scholarly study indexed online quotes an **1819** petition and identifies **A. Barbi, `La Festa dei Ceri ...`, testo VI, pp. 130–138** as an edition of the 1851 statute. Its PDF was not accessible in the source-reader, so this is a **bibliographic lead only**; the actual statute, quoted petition and pages were **not read or independently certified**. No claimant should elevate these references to primary-document access.

**Access route:** Original private archive held by **Università dei Muratori**, consultation **on request through the Soprintendenza archivistica e bibliografica dell'Umbria** (SIUSA lists `sab-umb@cultura.gov.it`). No request has been sent, no reproduction ordered.

### Salemi: tracing a historical 1779 St Nicholas bread expenditure

The [Salemi Ritual Bread Museum description](https://museodelpaneritualesalemi.jimdofree.com/pani-salemitani-bread-of-salemi/) reports that a **1779 Augustinian account register** at the State Archives of Trapani records expenses for `Panelli di San Nicolò da Tolentino`. This is an **unverified museum attribution**, not an original folio inspected by this research stream; the **precise archival busta, register title and page are not established**.

The Italian National Archival System [SIAS inventory for Trapani](https://sias-archivi.cultura.gov.it/cgi-bin/pagina.pl?ApriNodo=0&Chiave=519220&ChiaveAlbero=605891&ChiaveRadice=519220&RicProgetto=as-trapani&RicSez=complessi&RicTipoScheda=ca&RicVM=indice&TipoPag=comparc) confirms a `Corporazioni religiose soppresse` **institutional fund** of **1,263 units, 1402–1899**, organized partly by Salemi and by religious house, and containing cashbooks, income/expense ledgers, records of Masses, and property documents. **This confirms a plausible holdings route, not that the target 1779 manuscript has been personally located.** A separate nineteenth-century `Commissioni circondariali per l'enfiteusi` index contains **Salemi Convento S. Agostino, busta 8, fascicolo 54** relating to property; it may support identifying the institution but is **not the 1779 bread ledger** and must not be conflated with it.


**Additional finding-aid page verification:** The State Archives' [2019 *Commissione provinciale per la liquidazione dell'asse ecclesiastico* inventory](https://archiviodistatotrapani.cultura.gov.it/fileadmin/risorse/file_cartella_vecchio_sito/18._Commissione_provinciale_per_la_liquidazione_dell_asse_ecclesiastico.pdf) was visually inspected at PDF pages **37–38** (zero-based page indices **36–37**, corresponding to printed inventory pp. **35–36**). It explicitly lists **Salemi Convento di S. Agostino**, **1868**, **archival busta 18**, **cases 42–43**, with multiple parcel/lot entries. This is concrete evidence of nineteenth-century institutional property administration, not the 1779 bread account. Do not confuse the finding-aid's busta 18 with a shelfmark for the earlier accounting book.

**Access route:** The [Archivio di Stato di Trapani](https://archiviodistatotrapani.cultura.gov.it/istituto) provides distance reference enquiries; `as-tp@cultura.gov.it` is its published address. No request, booking or transaction has been made.

## Original public-domain printed books as independent chronology checks

The bibliographic record identifies:
- Giuseppe Pitrè, [*Spettacoli e feste popolari siciliane*](https://books.google.it/books?id=HmGBAAAAMAAJ), **1881**, 475 pages, available as a digital edition.
- Giuseppe Pitrè, [*Feste patronali in Sicilia*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Feste_patronali_in_Sicilia_descritte_da.html?id=RvGBAAAAMAAJ), **1900**, 572 pages.
- Giuseppe Pitrè, *Usi e costumi, credenze e pregiudizi del popolo siciliano*, **1889**, four volumes ([original edition catalogue](https://www.archivumdoc.it/2019/02/usi-e-costumi-credenze-e-pregiudizi-del-popolo-siciliano-vol-1-4-raccolti-e-descritti-da-giuseppe-pitre/)).

These have been **bibliographically located**, **not line-by-line read** or matched to existing Sicilian customs. They may be the most economical way to secure earlier evidence for multiple local customs rather than continuing to subdivide contemporary online descriptions. Pitrè himself compiled observations from multiple informants and printed predecessors; a printed entry therefore does not automatically prove independent eyewitness authority. Once original pages become accessible, record book, original edition, page and the exact level of attribution and period. Keep superstitious beliefs quarantined.
