# CPDV high-priority passage decisions — Catholic source-language review

**9 October 2026 · Evidence examined against the maintained original CPDV, the pinned Douay–Rheims Catholic transcription, Clementine Latin and, where indicated, Greek.**

This is a substantive **editorial adjudication pass**, not an imprimatur, not a Church judgment, and not an approval to publish the entire CPDV Bible. Text below is a comparison of the original author's wording, not an edited replacement for that translation. Assigning a critical note does not license modifying the original author's verse.

The inversion is Vulgate-literal; with v.2 it need not deny the personal distinction between Word and Father. But ordinary English may hear an exclusive identification. D–R's *“the Word was God”* conveys the doctrinal proposition more straightforwardly. | **Meaning can be orthodox; flag significant English ambiguity**, compare vv.1–3 in any critical note. Marian doctrine is still explicitly affirmed by the rest of CPDV 1:43. |

The Catholic traditional wording is *sorcerers* (also historically poisoners/magic practitioners), not simply the modern behavioral category *drug abusers*. | **High-priority meaning shift**. A reader could misunderstand what John is condemning; flag as doctrinally material semantic risk, not evidence that modern drug misuse is morally good. |

## Editorial verdicts

The **strongest unresolved semantic risks** are (1) Sirach 24:1's *mind* instead of *self/soul*, (2) Revelation's *drug abusers* versus traditional *sorcerers*, and (3) the sense of Elizabeth's exclamation in Luke 1:43. John 1:1 requires **contextual doctrinal caution** but follows the actual Latin and should not be summarily labelled heretical. Matthew 5:28 and 1 Corinthians 11:27 are mainly modern-English quality problems, directly relevant to the user's choice of CPDV for easier reading.

A Bible can be historically public domain and source-accurate without all translation decisions being suitable for uncomplicated catechetical use. Do not silently revise CPDV and label it the original author's text. When CPDV is eventually enabled, significant disputed renderings should have a concise, optional, sourced **comparison with Douay–Rheims and Vulgate**. No distracting note should interrupt normal liturgical or devotional reading.

## Remaining independent gates

- Complete per-verse or book-level source provenance for D–R, CPDV and Crampon; author-master checksum change detection.
- Independent Catholic textual/theological peer review, especially the semantic issues above, rather than a procedural or automatic certification of the entire Bible.
- Any passage-level crosswalk not individually checked stays disabled (particularly nonexception Psalms and other differing Vulgate conventions).
- All full-text `SCRIPTURE_EDITIONS[*].enabled` controls remain false. The 73 author-master CPDV JSON files are currently **review-only staging**, not app-visible biblical text.
