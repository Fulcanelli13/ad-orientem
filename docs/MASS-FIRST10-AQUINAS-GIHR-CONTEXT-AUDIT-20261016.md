## What is independently established at this stage

The first two loci are the exact Latin response records in the Corpus Thomisticum edition/transcription of Thomas Aquinas's *Summa Theologiae* III, q. 83, a. 5:

- **AO.MASS.WORK.AQUINAS.L01**, `ad 3` — Corpus Thomisticum paragraph **[51178]**, existing claims VER-064–068. The text enumerates the allegorical meanings of repeated signs of the Cross in the Mass. His symbolism must not be presented as the historical origin of those gestures or as independent normative rules for a 1962 Missal profile.
- **AO.MASS.WORK.AQUINAS.L02**, `ad 4` — Corpus Thomisticum **[51179]**, existing claim VER-069. Post-consecration crosses are explained as commemorating the power of Christ's Cross and the mode of the Passion, not as re-consecrating or blessing Christ.

**Latin text witness**: https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/sth4083.html . Page states it represents a 1906 Leonine Latin edition transcribed by Busa and subsequently revised by Alarcón. These are checked **digital-text locators**, not collated printed-page photographic evidence, verified translations or permissions to reproduce the whole article.

The remaining eight contexts are in the digitized **1902 B. Herder English witness** of Nikolaus Gihr, *The Holy Sacrifice of the Mass*, translated from the **sixth German edition** (confirmed on the transcription's title page). Original item: https://archive.org/details/holysacrificeofm00gihriala . Research OCR transcription: https://archive.org/stream/holysacrificeofm00gihriala/holysacrificeofm00gihriala_djvu.txt .

- **Gihr L01**: §59, p. 628; supreme blessing, lexical disagreements on `adscripta`, and `ratam`.
- **Gihr L02**: §59, pp. 627–630; overall `Quam oblationem` five-adjective interpretive structure.
- **Gihr L03**: §59, pp. 629–630; `rationabilis` and source-note allusion to Romans 12:1.
- **Gihr L04**: §61, p. 660; Melchisedech typology **context found**, but the cross-reference to **pp. 75–76 is still unchecked**. It is a deliberately *partial* context verification.
- **Gihr L05**: §61, p. 659; Abel and Abraham/Isaac typology, with authorial conjecture about heavenly fire explicitly not stated by Genesis.
- **Gihr L06**: §68, p. 717; `Ecce Agnus Dei` and St John the Baptist.
- **Gihr L07**: §68, pp. 715–716; development from three petitions for mercy to the final petition for peace. This historical commentary is **not an unqualified 1962 liturgical rule**.
- **Gihr L08**: §68, pp. 715–717; the `Agnus Dei` invocation and historical litany practice.

The OCR typography has several errors and a precise scanned original would need to be inspected to authenticate every printed word and page. **No full original scanned PDF page was available for a reliable side-by-side original-image collation in this pass**. The 1902 translation and modern digitization are separate evidence layers; original German text and edition variant history remain uncollated. A digital OCR context match does not certify the translation or prove the claim's exact historical scope.
