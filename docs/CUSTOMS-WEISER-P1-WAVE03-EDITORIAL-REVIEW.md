### 1. The family Advent wreath / La couronne de l’Avent en famille

**Occasion:** The four Sundays of Advent  
**Attested scope:** Catholic household use; adopted devotional form rather than an original ancient Roman rite  
**Owner:** Calendar / family customs, not the 1962 Mass reader

**English — custom:** A ring of evergreen branches with four candles can accompany a family's Advent prayers. One more candle is lighted each Sunday, marking the approach of Christmas and the expectation of Christ's coming.

**Meaning:** Its growing light reminds the household of hope and preparation for the Saviour.

**Optional observance:** Light the appropriate candle during a short family prayer or Scripture reading, using a safe candle arrangement. The devotion is optional; do not imply the Church prescribes it at a 1962 Mass.

**Français — coutume :** Une couronne de feuillage portant quatre bougies peut accompagner la prière familiale durant l'Avent. Chaque dimanche, une bougie supplémentaire est allumée pour marquer l'approche de Noël et l'attente du Christ.

**Sens :** Sa lumière grandissante rappelle l'espérance et la préparation à la venue du Sauveur.

**Pratique libre :** Allumer la bougie correspondante pendant une brève prière ou lecture de l'Écriture, en veillant à la sécurité. Cette dévotion est libre et ne constitue pas une rubrique de la messe de 1962.

**Weiser:** ch. 5, printed p. 55 (`WEI58-05-002`).  
**Independent source:** [United States Conference of Catholic Bishops](https://www.usccb.org/prayers/blessing-advent-wreath).

**Release restriction:** The USCCB verifies accepted Catholic household practice; its source does not settle Weiser's historical claim about Protestant origins.

### 2. Praying together at the Christmas crib / Prier en famille devant la crèche

**Occasion:** During Advent and Christmas Eve  
**Attested scope:** Roman Catholic domestic devotion; local crèche customs differ  
**Owner:** Calendar / Learn; reuse existing prayers

**English — custom:** The family crèche offers a place for children and adults to contemplate the Nativity. Catholic guidance recommends gathering before the crib to hear the birth narrative, sing a carol and pray together.

**Meaning:** It brings the Gospel account of Bethlehem into the ordinary life of the home.

**Optional observance:** Read Luke 2 together, pray in thanksgiving and, if customary in the family, place the figure of the Christ Child in the crib at Christmas.

**Français — coutume :** La crèche familiale invite enfants et adultes à contempler la Nativité. Les directives catholiques encouragent à s'y réunir pour entendre le récit de la naissance de Jésus, chanter un cantique et prier ensemble.

**Sens :** Elle fait entrer le récit évangélique de Bethléem dans la vie ordinaire du foyer.

**Pratique libre :** Lire ensemble Luc 2, rendre grâce et, si tel est l'usage de la famille, placer la figure de l'Enfant Jésus dans la crèche à Noël.

**Weiser:** ch. 6, printed p. 74 (`WEI58-06-007`).  
**Independent source:** [Holy See — Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, n. 109](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20020513_vers-direttorio_en.html).

**Release restriction:** Household prayer is not an extra liturgical ceremony; alpine diorama details and other geographical claims remain separate.

### 3. The Irish Christmas candle / La bougie de Noël dans les foyers irlandais

**Occasion:** Christmas Eve and Christmas night; some places retained candles for more days  
**Attested scope:** Historical Irish households, including documented Cork and Kerry localities  
**Owner:** Customs Atlas, Irish historical/regional example

**English — custom:** Irish folklore archives describe households lighting a candle on Christmas Eve, often at a window as a sign of welcome and remembrance. Some families associated Christmas candles with prayers for relatives who had died.

**Meaning:** The small light can recall Christ's birth, hospitality and prayer for loved ones.

**Optional observance:** As an optional family remembrance, place a safe light near a window and pray for relatives and for travellers; use an LED candle when safer.

**Français — coutume :** Les archives folkloriques irlandaises décrivent des familles allumant une bougie la veille de Noël, souvent à la fenêtre en signe d'accueil et de souvenir. Certaines y associaient la prière pour les proches défunts.

**Sens :** Cette lumière peut rappeler la naissance du Christ, l'hospitalité et la prière pour ceux que l'on aime.

**Pratique libre :** À titre de souvenir familial facultatif, placer une lumière sans danger près d'une fenêtre et prier pour les proches et les voyageurs ; préférer une bougie électrique si nécessaire.

**Weiser:** ch. 8, printed p. 96 (`WEI58-08-004`); ch. 8, printed p. 97 (`WEI58-08-008`).  
**Independent source:** [National Museum of Ireland — Christmas in years gone by; National Folklore Collection UCD](https://source.museum.ie/en-IE/Collections-Research/Folklife-Collections/Folklife-Collections-List-%281%29/Religion-and-Calendar-Customs/Generations-past-how-Christmas-was-celebrated-in-y).

**Release restriction:** Do not promote folkloric beliefs in automatic protection, the return of spirits or documented migration of this custom to the United States.

### 4. Proclaiming the date of Easter at Epiphany / L’annonce de la date de Pâques à l’Épiphanie

**Occasion:** 6 January in the 1962 Roman calendar; current calendars vary by jurisdiction  
**Attested scope:** Historical Latin ecclesiastical custom; the USCCB verifies modern use  
**Owner:** Calendar / Learn; reuse authoritative date calculation

**English — custom:** In an older Church custom, the date of Easter and the year's principal movable feasts are announced at Epiphany, reminding the faithful that the Resurrection orders the whole liturgical year.

**Meaning:** The feast of Christ's manifestation points toward His Paschal mystery, the centre of Christian worship.

**Optional observance:** Explore the year's feast dates in Ad Orientem's traditional Calendar; attend the proclamation only where a parish actually uses it.

**Français — coutume :** Selon un ancien usage de l'Église, on annonce à l'Épiphanie la date de Pâques et des principales fêtes mobiles de l'année, rappelant que la Résurrection structure toute l'année liturgique.

**Sens :** La manifestation du Christ oriente vers son mystère pascal, centre du culte chrétien.

**Pratique libre :** Consulter les dates de l'année dans le calendrier traditionnel d'Ad Orientem ; assister à la proclamation uniquement là où une paroisse la pratique.

**Weiser:** ch. 12, printed p. 148 (`WEI58-12-005`).  
**Independent source:** [United States Conference of Catholic Bishops](https://www.usccb.org/prayer-and-worship/liturgical-year-and-calendar/christmas/announcement-of-easter-and-the-moveable-feasts).

**Release restriction:** No copied modern formula presented as the correct 1962 proclamation, and no second feast-date engine.

### 5. Keeping and shaping blessed Palm Sunday branches / Garder et disposer les rameaux bénits

**Occasion:** Palm Sunday and the following days of Holy Week  
**Attested scope:** French Catholic regional branches and a separately documented modern household cross-making activity  
**Owner:** Existing DOM-013 / Calendar

**English — custom:** Catholics often take blessed branches home after Palm Sunday. Depending on locality they may be palm, olive, box or other greenery. Some families shape simple crosses from the fronds to recall Christ's entry into Jerusalem and His approaching Passion.

**Meaning:** The branches witness to faith in Christ, rather than serving as protective talismans.

**Optional observance:** Keep a blessed branch reverently near a crucifix; a small decorative palm cross is optional. Participate first in the Church's procession where it is celebrated.

**Français — coutume :** Après le dimanche des Rameaux, les fidèles rapportent chez eux des branches bénites, de palmier, d'olivier, de buis ou d'autres végétaux selon les régions. Certaines familles en forment une petite croix pour évoquer l'entrée de Jésus à Jérusalem et sa Passion.

**Sens :** Les rameaux témoignent de la foi au Christ et ne sont pas des talismans protecteurs.

**Pratique libre :** Conserver avec respect un rameau bénit près d'un crucifix ; une petite croix décorative en palme est facultative. Participer d'abord à la procession de l'Église là où elle a lieu.

**Weiser:** ch. 15, printed p. 191 (`WEI58-15-006`); ch. 15, printed p. 191 (`WEI58-15-007`).  
**Independent source:** [Église catholique en France — Semaine Sainte et Pâques](https://eglise.catholique.fr/espace-presse/dossiers-de-presse/368505-semaine-sainte-et-paques/); [Catholic Culture — Blessed Palms in the Home](https://www.catholicculture.org/culture/liturgicalyear/activities/view.cfm?id=1035).

**Release restriction:** The blessing belongs to the minister; home crafts are not mandated, and magical uses are excluded. This is an enrichment of existing DOM-013, not a new canonical custom.

### 6. The Holy Week processions of Popayán / Les processions de la Semaine sainte à Popayán

**Occasion:** Holy Week in the Colombian city of Popayán  
**Attested scope:** Specific UNESCO-documented Catholic community in Colombia  
**Owner:** Customs Atlas / Explore geographical historical context

**English — custom:** In Popayán, Holy Week processions carry richly decorated religious images recalling episodes of the Passion, the burial of Christ and the Resurrection. The local community preserves the customs, processional floats and associated craftsmanship.

**Meaning:** The visible story of Christ's Passion fosters recollection, communal prayer and transmission between generations.

**Optional observance:** When in a participating locality, consult the authentic programme and attend respectfully; otherwise meditate on the Gospel account of the Passion.

**Français — coutume :** À Popayán, les processions de la Semaine sainte portent des images religieuses ornées rappelant la Passion, la mise au tombeau du Christ et la Résurrection. La communauté conserve les chars, les gestes et l'artisanat qui accompagnent ces cérémonies.

**Sens :** Le récit visible de la Passion encourage le recueillement, la prière commune et la transmission entre générations.

**Pratique libre :** Dans une localité où cette tradition existe, consulter le programme officiel et participer avec respect ; autrement, méditer le récit évangélique de la Passion.

**Weiser:** ch. 15, printed p. 201 (`WEI58-15-025`).  
**Independent source:** [UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage — Popayán Holy Week processions](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/holy-week-processions-in-popayan-00259).

**Release restriction:** UNESCO verifies Popayán; it does not prove identical customs across all Spain or Latin America or add them to the Mass reader.

### 7. Penitents and confraternities in Spanish Holy Week / Pénitents et confréries de la Semaine sainte espagnole

**Occasion:** Holy Week, particularly Andalusian processions  
**Attested scope:** Andalusia, Spain; role and costume differ among brotherhoods  
**Owner:** Customs Atlas / Learn regional historical context

**English — custom:** In Andalusian Holy Week, confraternities accompany processional images of Christ and Our Lady. Many members wear tunics and pointed penitential hoods known as capirotes, taking part in an organised act of public devotion.

**Meaning:** The penitential clothing belongs to a specific Spanish Christian confraternity tradition.

**Optional observance:** Where the practice is local and properly organised, join or attend reverently; it is not a costume or rite the app asks worshippers to reproduce.

**Français — coutume :** Pendant la Semaine sainte andalouse, des confréries accompagnent les images du Christ et de Notre-Dame en procession. De nombreux membres portent des tuniques et des capuchons pénitentiels pointus, les capirotes, dans le cadre d'une dévotion publique organisée.

**Sens :** Ce vêtement pénitentiel appartient à une tradition précise des confréries chrétiennes espagnoles.

**Pratique libre :** Là où la tradition est organisée localement, y participer ou y assister avec respect ; l'application ne demande pas de reproduire le costume ou le rite.

**Weiser:** ch. 15, printed p. 203 (`WEI58-15-028`).  
**Independent source:** [Turismo de Andalucía — Términos de Semana Santa](https://www.andalucia.org/eventos/semana-santa/terminos-de-semana-santa/).

**Release restriction:** Regional culture is not a universal Roman ceremonial rubric; no inferred identity with unrelated modern costume movements.

### 8. Easter eggs as a sign of new life / Les œufs de Pâques, signe de vie nouvelle

**Occasion:** Easter and its family celebrations  
**Attested scope:** Christian seasonal symbolic custom; specific historical Roman Ritual egg blessing requires separate authentication  
**Owner:** Calendar / Customs Atlas, no new Holy Saturday rite

**English — custom:** Eggs appear in many Easter household traditions as a symbol of life and renewal. Church guidance recognises the blessing of eggs among Easter popular devotions, while the exact practices vary by place.

**Meaning:** The symbol can point toward the new life Christ has opened through His Resurrection.

**Optional observance:** Share or decorate eggs with family in the Easter season; if a parish offers a proper priestly food blessing, attend according to its practice.

**Français — coutume :** Dans diverses traditions familiales de Pâques, les œufs figurent la vie et le renouveau. Les directives de l'Église mentionnent leur bénédiction parmi les dévotions pascales, avec des usages variables selon les lieux.

**Sens :** Ce symbole peut rappeler la vie nouvelle ouverte par la Résurrection du Christ.

**Pratique libre :** Partager ou décorer des œufs en famille au temps pascal ; si la paroisse propose une bénédiction sacerdotale des aliments, y participer selon son usage.

**Weiser:** ch. 17, printed p. 234 (`WEI58-17-010`).  
**Independent source:** [Holy See — Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, n. 150](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20020513_vers-direttorio_en.html).

**Release restriction:** No claim that a current Easter-egg blessing proves the precise 1962 Rituale Romanum formula; no separate rite in app.

### 9. Singing Veni Creator at Pentecost / Chanter le Veni Creator à la Pentecôte

**Occasion:** Pentecost and appropriate invocations of the Holy Spirit  
**Attested scope:** Universal Catholic hymn evidence for Pentecost; other particular ceremonies depend on proper source  
**Owner:** Existing Pray / Pentecost Learn, no music module

**English — custom:** The hymn Veni Creator Spiritus invokes the Holy Spirit's light and gifts. Pope John Paul II explicitly cited it as the Church's prayer at Pentecost.

**Meaning:** The faithful ask the Holy Spirit to renew their hearts and guide the Church.

**Optional observance:** Recite or sing the hymn if its existing text is available in a properly sourced prayer module; otherwise use the app's Pentecost prayers.

**Français — coutume :** L'hymne Veni Creator Spiritus invoque la lumière et les dons du Saint-Esprit. Saint Jean-Paul II l'a expressément évoquée comme prière de l'Église à la Pentecôte.

**Sens :** Les fidèles demandent au Saint-Esprit de renouveler leurs cœurs et de conduire l'Église.

**Pratique libre :** Réciter ou chanter l'hymne si son texte authentifié se trouve déjà dans les prières ; autrement, utiliser les prières de Pentecôte de l'application.

**Weiser:** ch. 19, printed p. 250 (`WEI58-19-009`).

### 10. Decorating for the Corpus Christi procession / Orner les fenêtres pour la Fête-Dieu

**Occasion:** Corpus Christi, where a Eucharistic procession is locally organised  
**Attested scope:** Catholic popular practice documented in a Holy See directory; decoration varies locally  
**Owner:** Customs Atlas / Calendar feast-day context, separate from procession state

**English — custom:** Catholic communities sometimes decorate the streets and windows along a Eucharistic processional route with flowers. These offerings frame a public act of reverence for Christ present in the Blessed Sacrament.

**Meaning:** The house and street decorations help direct attention to the Eucharistic Lord rather than to a competition of displays.

**Optional observance:** If a procession passes your home, offer simple suitable flowers or join the parish's decoration work after asking its organisers.

**Français — coutume :** Des communautés catholiques fleurissent parfois les rues et les fenêtres le long du passage de la procession eucharistique. Ces ornements accompagnent un acte public de révérence envers le Christ présent dans le Saint-Sacrement.

**Sens :** Les ornements des maisons et des rues invitent à tourner le regard vers le Seigneur eucharistique, et non à rivaliser de décorations.

**Pratique libre :** Si une procession passe devant chez soi, disposer sobrement des fleurs ou aider l'équipe paroissiale avec l'accord des organisateurs.

**Weiser:** ch. 21, printed p. 262 (`WEI58-21-007`).  
**Independent source:** [Holy See — Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, n. 162](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20020513_vers-direttorio_en.html).

**Release restriction:** This source documents flowers, not specifically Weiser's candles and sacred images. No presumed procession in the 1962 reader.
