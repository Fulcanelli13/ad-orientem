# R18 — Eleven Customs: editorial decision and publication-lane audit

**Reviewed:** 30 October 2026. **Owner:** [PR #1759](https://github.com/Fulcanelli13/ad-orientem/pull/1759), with reader-only comparison UI in [PR #2024](https://github.com/Fulcanelli13/ad-orientem/pull/2024). **Status: proposed, not approved.** The canonical Calendar remains at 35 published Customs.

## Outcome

The editorial review reduces **eleven draft records** into **seven proposed regional, source-backed historical Customs articles** and **four locality-bound historical accounts**. A locally announced bonfire or named procession must not be turned into a global recommendation, scheduled event, Roman rite, or mandatory household custom. The seven article candidates can be considered for region-filtered Calendar prose *only after* complete source and bilingual signoff. There is no new generic “Customs Atlas” and no separate parent article for thematic groupings.

The complete eight-field English/French candidate source was corrected a further **27 paired fields across all eleven articles**. All before/after values are retained in [the QA amendments file](../data/customs/customs-r18-eleven-bilingual-qa-amendments.v1.json), with a deterministic test checking exact reconstruction of the final [source-checked candidate pack](../data/customs/customs-r18-eleven-source-checked-full-bilingual-article-pack.v1.json). The [publication-lane register](../data/customs/customs-r18-eleven-editorial-publication-lanes.v1.json) stores the distinct proposed outcome per action and all eight false approval gates.

## Editorial corrections that materially affect accuracy

1. **R18-03 Saint John:** English and French occasions now agree on association with the Nativity of Saint John the Baptist (24 June) while the Oloron **2026 event occurred on 20 June**. No automatic 23 June vigil or 24 June parish bonfire is suggested. The exact 2026 programme is [diocesan](https://diocese64.org/evenement/feu-de-la-saint-jean-a-oloron-sainte-marie/).
2. **R18-04 Easter eggs:** Polish **pisanki** and Ukrainian **pysanky** remain cultural subtypes of *one* decoration action. [UNESCO's 2024 inscription](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/pysanka-ukrainian-tradition-and-art-of-decorating-eggs-02134) documents a cross-confessional craft and reports the folk use of **already blessed** eggs. The corrected caution distinguishes decorating an egg from having an egg blessed; folklore about protective efficacy is reported as folklore, not erased or promoted into Catholic doctrine.
3. **R18-05 Herbergsuche:** text, title and occasion now present the [Austrian parish's 2023 image-relay practice](https://www.pfarre-grosspetersdorf.at/pfarre/1131/aktuelles/article/12277.html) without prescribing a universal novena or asserting a current parish route.
4. **R18-07 Guignolée:** [the organiser](https://ssvp-mtl.org/fr/guignolee) explicitly dates the modern Québec practice to **1861** and describes collecting donations before Christmas. The corrected history acknowledges the older New Year house-visiting origin without flattening it into exclusively Catholic-liturgical practice.
5. **R18-08/09 Polish/Hungarian Christmas plays:** internal editorial language (“additional canonical Customs”) is removed from the public-facing copy, and “quêteurs” is replaced with French wording about **visiting singers**. These remain distinct national practices with subordinate dramatic forms.
6. **R18-13 L-Irxoxt:** the heading no longer implies all Maltese Resurrection processions involve **running**. Senglea's running is a local subtype of a broader documented Easter procession, not a Mass rubric.
7. **R18-14 Polish Pentecost greenery:** the unsupported archival-photograph caption was **removed from the candidate history and scope**, not passed through to Calendar users as an unresolved editing footnote. The historical domestic/gate greenery is supported by the [Polish State Forests account](https://www.lasy.gov.pl/pl/edukacja/blogi/blog-lesniczego/zielone-swiatki-2013-nie-zapominajmy-o-nich).
8. **R18-15 Tivoli Inchinata:** the [diocese's 2026 programme](https://diocesitivoliepalestrina.it/festa-dellinchinata-2026/) establishes a 14 August encounter and 15 August renewed greeting. Its timing is no longer rendered as an unqualified perpetual date, and a traditional foundation legend is not presented as proven origin.
9. **R18-16 Sassari Faradda:** municipal records describe guilds carrying large **wooden candelieri**; the date is carefully identified as the **2026** programme, not a universal Marian vigil rite.
10. **R18-17 Frauendreißiger:** the named Bavarian period is now described as a **Marian pilgrimage period**, rather than a translated fixed “thirty days” that could be mistaken for 30 individually prescribed prayers. Its usual 15 August–14 September association is not a new season in the 1962 Calendar. Maria Thalkirchen [reports a papal indulgence granted in 1780](https://www.mariathalkirchen.de/index.php?id=127); the original document and current conditions remain unverified, so no indulgence has been published.

## Independent release gates

Every record remains `human final copy = false` and holds distinct `identity`, `source`, `english`, `french`, `geography`, `liturgical`, `calendar`, `activation` approvals. Source corroboration of a limited historical assertion is **not** equivalent to final signoff for a full article. No canonical ID is allocated. No active or future local date is published. No licit celebrant action or 1962 Missal rubric is inferred. No indulgence conditions are offered.

If the seven recommended regional identities ultimately clear editorial signoff, allocate IDs and integrate them **individually through the canonical existing Customs seed and Calendar read model**, with 1962 feast/season resolver and region preferences. The four local histories should remain contextual narratives attached to a *locally verified* feast where relevant, not global new Customs entries. Prior six proposed R18 IDs and independent two-entry PR #1642 remain separate release cohorts; do not double count or merge them implicitly.
