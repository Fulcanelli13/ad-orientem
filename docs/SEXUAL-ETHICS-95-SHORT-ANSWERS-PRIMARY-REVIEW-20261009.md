## Substantive corrections in the public English/French answers

1. **CSE057 and CSE077:** do not turn a catalogue of particular acts or artificial-intelligence imagery into an invented magisterial decree. The ordinary *Catechism* §2354 pornography definition and chastity norms have different scopes; applications to generative images are reasoned, not quoted.
2. **CSE097 and CSE100:** *Dignitas Infinita* §60 expressly distinguishes treatment of congenital and subsequently developed genital abnormalities from its ordinary sex-change category. Its anthropology is not a pediatric treatment algorithm.
3. **CSE108 and CSE110:** a diagnostic aim does not make any method of specimen collection morally neutral. *Dignitas Personae* §19 treats proposed prenatal adoption to rescue frozen embryos as praiseworthy in motive but ethically difficult, without proclaiming a universally acceptable solution.
4. **CSE119 and CSE120:** survivors of abuse must not be instructed to confess involuntary suffering as personal sin. Freezing, threatened submission and involuntary physiological response cannot be used as proof of free consent. *Canon 988* concerns sins actually committed by the penitent; the source-to-survivor application is an explicitly labelled inference.
5. **CSE131, CSE133–135:** [CIC can. 988](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib4-cann959-997_en.html) requires kind and number; [can. 979](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib4-cann959-997_en.html) directs confessors to prudence and discretion, not intrusive graphic questioning; can. 987 requires a present firm purpose of amendment. Approximate counting is labelled pastoral inference, **not literal canonical text**.
6. **CSE139–140:** [can. 916](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib4-cann879-958_en.html) requires **both** a grave reason and no opportunity to confess for the narrow exception, together with perfect contrition and intent to confess promptly. The chastity programme cannot imply the Eucharist may always be received without regard to grave sin.
7. **CSE144, CSE150:** mercy and culpability are distinguished from the moral object. The positive vision explicitly includes chaste celibacy as well as marriage.
