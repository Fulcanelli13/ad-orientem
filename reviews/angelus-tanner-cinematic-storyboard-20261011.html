Artwork: Henry Ossawa Tanner, The Annunciation (1898), Philadelphia Museum of Art, W1899-1-1. This preview references a Wikimedia Commons Public Domain Mark reproduction; not a museum-site download.

Cinematic principle

Advance only on user action. Stage I discovers the column of light; II tracks into Mary's response; III recovers the beam for the Incarnation; IV settles on Mary. The camera is still while praying. The production reader—not this isolated visual storyboard—remains authoritative for exact text, translations, responses and posture.

Approval criteria

Review the cropping of Mary's face and hands at each stage and whether Gabriel's light remains legible. The Incarnation must never puppet the figure or show a manufactured dove. The original Angelus has three Hail Marys; the closing stage has its final versicle and collect, not a fourth. On Sunday or after Saturday Vespers the source posture logic still applies.
